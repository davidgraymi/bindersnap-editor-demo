The Solution

You already have the policies.
What you need is the proof.

Word, PDF, whatever the last person left behind — Bindersnap versions and approves your files exactly as they are.

Store

Every Policy, One Place

Every policy gets its own page, its own version history, and its own approval record. Point your staff at one link instead of a folder they have to guess their way through. No migration project, no consultant.

Approve

Sign-Off That Cannot Be Back-Dated

Every approval is stamped with the person, the date, and the version they actually read. Approved versions lock — a revision arrives as a new version with its own review, never as a quiet edit to the one already signed.

Produce

Evidence While They Wait

Asked who approved the current version and when? It is on the page, in front of you, while the surveyor is still in the room. Asked for the whole history? Every version is there in order, each with the sign-offs it collected — no support ticket, no vendor in the middle.

The Record

An approval nobody can
quietly edit — including us.

Most policy systems keep an approval as a row in their database. A row can be changed by anyone with the right access, and you would never know. Change ours and everyone can see it.

Sealed

Every version gets a fingerprint

When a version is approved, it is sealed with a fingerprint calculated from its exact contents. Change one word after the fact and the fingerprint no longer matches. There is no quiet edit.

Linked

Each entry seals the one before it

The record is a chain. Every entry carries the fingerprint of the entry before it, so a step cannot be removed, re-dated, or slipped in without breaking every link that follows.

Open

Not rows in our database

The record is kept as ordinary commits and plain files — the formats version control has used for twenty years. It is not a private schema that only we know how to read.

The Review

The review happens on the policy.
Not in your inbox.

Questions get raised on the version they are about, and the answer stays attached to it. What has to happen before a version reaches the floor is your call — and once you set it, the system holds the line instead of the person who remembers to.

Threaded

Every question stays with its version

The Medical Director raises a point, the author replies underneath, and the whole exchange stays attached to the version it was raised against. Six months later it still reads in order.

Enforced

Set the rule once. It holds.

Require two approvals, or the Medical Director specifically. Refuse to publish while a question is still open. Clear old sign-offs whenever a new version arrives. You pick which rules apply; after that nobody has to remember them.

Recorded

The publish gate keeps its own history

The rule that holds a version back while a question is open is stored as a committed file, not a checkbox in a database. Turn it on or off and that change is a commit, with a name and a date on it.

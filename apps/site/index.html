The Record

Nobody can say you fixed it.

Once a version is approved, nobody can change it after the fact without it showing — not your staff, not you, and not us. When the surveyor asks, the record answers for itself, and no one can claim it was tidied up the night before the visit.

The Review

The review happens on the policy.
Not in your inbox.

Questions get raised on the version they are about, and the answer stays attached to it. What has to happen before a version reaches the floor is your call — and once you set it, the system holds the line instead of the person who remembers to.

Threaded

Every question stays with its version

The Medical Director raises a point, the author replies underneath, and the whole exchange stays attached to the version it was raised against. Six months later it still reads in order.

Enforced

Set the rule once. It holds.

Require 3 of your 5 board members, or the Medical Director specifically. Refuse to publish while a question is still open. Clear old sign-offs whenever a new version arrives. You pick which rules apply; after that nobody has to remember them.

Recorded

The publish gate keeps its own history

The rule that holds a version back while a question is open keeps its own log. Turn it on or off and the change is recorded with who made it and when.

Questions

What people ask before they start.

What is Bindersnap?

Bindersnap is policy and procedure management software for small healthcare providers. Every policy keeps its current approved version, every earlier version, and the record of who approved each one and when. When a surveyor asks which version you approved, the answer is on the page, not buried in an email thread.

Who is Bindersnap for?

Small healthcare providers who keep a policy manual and answer to a surveyor or accreditor: ambulatory surgery centers, physician practices and clinics, rural health clinics, home health agencies, hospices and behavioral health programs. It is built for the person who runs approvals today through Word, email and a shared drive.

How much does Bindersnap cost?

$39 a month for each person who writes and publishes policies: owners, admins and editors. Reviewers who approve changes and staff who only read are free, in any number. Every organization starts with a 14-day free trial, and you don’t need a card to start.

How does a policy get approved?

Someone proposes a change, the approvers you name review it, and only then does it become the current version. You set the rule for each binder: how many approvals a change needs, and whose count, such as 3 of your 5 board members. Questions are raised on the version they are about, and the answers stay with it. An approved version cannot change without a new version that goes through review.

Can we keep writing our policies in Word?

Yes. Upload the Word and PDF files you already have, and each new version goes through the same review and approval. Or write them in Bindersnap’s own editor, which works like Word and runs in your browser, so nobody needs a Word license. Either way, every version and the people who approved it stay on record.

Does Bindersnap store patient information?

No. Bindersnap holds your organization’s policies and procedures, not patient records or other protected health information. Keep patient information in your clinical systems.

What happens to our policies if we stop paying?

They stay. Reading and exporting don’t need a subscription, for as long as your organization keeps its account. Everyone can still open every document, see who approved each version, and download it. Nobody can propose, approve or publish new changes until someone subscribes.

How long does it take to set up?

An afternoon for most small providers: create your organization, upload the policies you already have, and name the people who approve them. There is no demo call, quote or implementation project. The getting started guide walks through moving a larger manual in.

Word, PDF, whatever the last person left behind — Bindersnap versions and approves your files exactly as they are.

The Record

A record the surveyor believes.
Nobody can say you fixed it.

Once a version is approved, nobody can change it after the fact without it showing — not your staff, not you, and not us. When the surveyor asks, the record answers for itself, and no one can claim it was tidied up the night before the visit.

  • Sealed

    Each version gets a fingerprint from its exact words. Change one word and the fingerprint changes with it.

  • Linked

    Every entry carries the fingerprint of the entry before it, so the order is part of the record too.

    Download the whole record as a PDF and a spreadsheet, any time. You never need us to read your own record.

The Review

The review happens on the policy.
Not in your inbox.

Questions get raised on the version they are about, and the answer stays attached to it. What has to happen before a version reaches the floor is your call — and once you set it, the system holds the line instead of the person who remembers to.

Threaded

Every question stays with its version

The Medical Director raises a point, the author replies underneath, and the whole exchange stays attached to the version it was raised against. Six months later it still reads in order.

Enforced

Set the rule once. It holds.

Require two approvals, or the Medical Director specifically. Refuse to publish while a question is still open. Clear old sign-offs whenever a new version arrives. You pick which rules apply; after that nobody has to remember them.

Recorded

The publish gate keeps its own history

The rule that holds a version back while a question is open keeps its own log. Turn it on or off and the change is recorded with who made it and when.

A Note From the Founder

My wife came home frustrated with her policy system often enough that I started asking questions. Reviewing a document meant opening it in Word, which not everyone had. Getting one approved meant a meeting, then an email chain, then keeping track of who had signed off on which draft.

I build software for a living, and I kept thinking: isn't this just how software teams approve changes? A change, the people who reviewed it, and the exact version they approved, all in one record. Engineers have worked this way for decades and stopped noticing it was ever hard. She did not need more software. She needed the thing my industry takes for granted.

So I built it for her.

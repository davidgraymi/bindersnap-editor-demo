Hand hygiene is the first policy an infection control surveyor reads and the first practice they watch. A **hand hygiene policy** should tell staff exactly when to clean their hands, which product to use, and how the organization checks that it happens.

CMS doesn't publish its own hand hygiene rules. Its infection control conditions, such as [§416.51](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-416.51) for ambulatory surgery centers, [§484.70](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-484.70) for home health and [§418.60](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-418.60) for hospice, require you to follow professionally acceptable or accepted standards of practice. For hand hygiene, the standard everyone measures against is [CDC's guidance for healthcare workers](https://www.cdc.gov/clean-hands/hcp/clinical-safety/index.html), which this template follows. Accreditors look for the same thing.

## What CDC's guidance says

| Topic              | CDC guidance                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| When               | Immediately before touching a patient; before an aseptic task; before moving from a soiled body site to a clean one on the same patient; after touching a patient or their surroundings; after contact with blood, body fluids or contaminated surfaces; immediately after removing gloves |
| Which product      | Alcohol-based hand sanitizer is preferred in most clinical situations unless hands are visibly soiled                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Soap and water     | When hands are visibly soiled; before eating; after using the restroom; during care of patients with suspected or confirmed infection during outbreaks of _C. difficile_ or norovirus                                                                                                      |
| Sanitizer strength | At least 60% alcohol                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| Technique          | Sanitizer: cover all surfaces and rub until dry, about 20 seconds. Soap and water: rub vigorously for at least 15 seconds                                                                                                                                                                  |
| Nails              | Natural nails should not extend past the fingertip; no artificial nails or extensions when in direct contact with high-risk patients                                                                                                                                                       |
| Gloves             | Gloves are not a substitute for hand hygiene                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |

## The template

### [Organization name] Hand Hygiene Policy

- **Policy owner:** [Infection Prevention Lead]
- **Approved by:** [Medical director, director of nursing, administrator]
- **Effective date:** [Date]
- **Last reviewed:** [Date]

### 1. Purpose

To prevent the spread of infection to patients and staff by setting out when and how everyone at [Organization name] cleans their hands, consistent with guidance from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.

### 2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees, providers, contracted staff, students and volunteers who have contact with patients, patient surroundings, or clean or sterile supplies, [in the facility and in patients' homes].

### 3. Policy

Staff perform hand hygiene:

1. Immediately before touching a patient.
2. Before an aseptic task, such as placing an indwelling device or handling an invasive medical device.
3. Before moving from a soiled body site to a clean body site on the same patient.
4. After touching a patient or the patient's immediate surroundings.
5. After contact with blood, body fluids or contaminated surfaces.
6. Immediately after removing gloves.

**Product.** Alcohol-based hand sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol is the preferred method unless hands are visibly soiled. Staff wash with soap and water when hands are visibly soiled, before eating, after using the restroom, and when caring for patients with suspected or confirmed _C. difficile_ or norovirus infection during an outbreak [or as directed by Infection Prevention].

**Gloves** do not replace hand hygiene. Staff clean their hands before putting gloves on when required by this policy, and immediately after removing them.

### 4. Procedure

**Alcohol-based hand sanitizer.** Apply the amount recommended by the manufacturer to the palm. Rub over all surfaces of hands and fingers until dry, about 20 seconds.

**Soap and water.** Wet hands with water, apply soap, and rub vigorously for at least 15 seconds, covering all surfaces of hands and fingers. Rinse, dry with a disposable towel, and use the towel to turn off the faucet.

### 5. Nails, jewelry and skin care

- Natural nails do not extend past the fingertip.
- Staff do not wear artificial nails or extensions when in direct contact with [high-risk patients, as defined by Infection Prevention: for example, surgical and procedural patients].
- [Rings and wrist jewelry: state your organization's rule.]
- Staff use only hand lotions supplied or approved by [Organization name], because other products can interfere with hand hygiene products. Staff with skin irritation report it to [Employee Health].

### 6. Supplies

Sanitizer dispensers are placed [at the point of care, at entrances to patient care areas, in each procedure room]. [For home care: each clinician carries sanitizer in their bag.] [Title] checks dispensers [frequency] and replaces expired product.

### 7. Monitoring and feedback

[Title] observes hand hygiene [number] times per [month] in [areas], using [direct observation by trained observers / product-volume monitoring]. Results are reported to [the QAPI committee] [monthly] and shared with staff. Individual coaching follows observed misses.

### 8. Education

Staff are trained on this policy at orientation and [annually].

### 9. References

Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, Clinical Safety: Hand Hygiene for Healthcare Workers. [Your accreditor's infection control standards.]

### Approval

Reviewed and approved by [names and titles] on [date].

## How to adapt it

- **Define "high-risk patients"** for the nail rule. Surgical, procedural and immunocompromised patients are the usual starting point.
- **Home health and hospice:** add how clinicians clean their hands in the home, including when there is no running water, and how the nursing bag is handled. Clean hands come before taking anything from the bag.
- **Set a monitoring target** your QAPI program can track, and report it the way the program reports everything else.

## Frequently asked questions

### What percentage of alcohol should hand sanitizer have in healthcare?

CDC recommends alcohol-based hand sanitizer that contains at least 60% alcohol.

### When must healthcare workers use soap and water instead of sanitizer?

When hands are visibly soiled, before eating, after using the restroom, and when caring for patients with suspected or confirmed _C. difficile_ or norovirus infection during outbreaks.

### Can staff wear artificial nails?

CDC advises against artificial fingernails or extensions for staff with direct contact with high-risk patients, and says natural nails should not extend past the fingertip. Many organizations ban artificial nails for all patient care staff.

CMS requires Medicare-certified providers to keep an **emergency preparedness program** with four parts: an emergency plan, policies and procedures, a communication plan, and a training and testing program. For ambulatory surgery centers the rule is [42 CFR §416.54](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-416.54); home health agencies (§484.102), hospices (§418.113) and rural health clinics and FQHCs (§491.12) have parallel sections with provider-specific details.

For these providers, **each of the four parts must be reviewed and updated at least every 2 years**, and surveyors check the dates. The template below follows the ASC rule, which is the plainest of them. If you are a home health agency or a hospice, read your own section too; both add requirements, for example about patients in their homes.

## What the rule requires

| Part                        | Key requirements (ASC, §416.54)                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Review                                  |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| (a) Emergency plan          | All-hazards risk assessment, facility-based and community-based; strategies for the events it identifies; patient population and services; continuity of operations, delegations of authority and succession; cooperation with emergency officials | At least every 2 years                  |
| (b) Policies and procedures | Tracking staff and sheltered patients; safe evacuation; shelter in place; medical documentation; volunteers and surge staffing; role under a section 1135 waiver                                                                                   | At least every 2 years                  |
| (c) Communication plan      | Contacts for staff, contractors, patients' physicians, volunteers and emergency officials; primary and alternate means of communication; sharing patient information as HIPAA permits                                                              | At least every 2 years                  |
| (d) Training and testing    | Initial training, training at least every 2 years, documentation; an exercise at least annually: a full-scale or functional exercise every 2 years and another exercise in the opposite year                                                       | Program reviewed at least every 2 years |

## The template

### [Organization name] Emergency Preparedness Program

- **Program owner:** [Title, for example Administrator]
- **Approved by:** [Governing body, administrator, medical director]
- **Effective date:** [Date]
- **Last reviewed:** [Date]
- **Next review due:** [No later than 2 years after last review]

### 1. Purpose and scope

This program prepares [Organization name] to protect patients, staff and visitors and to continue or safely suspend operations during emergencies, in accordance with 42 CFR §416.54 and applicable state and local requirements. It applies to all staff, individuals providing services under arrangement, and volunteers at [locations].

### 2. Emergency plan

**Risk assessment.** [Title] maintains a documented, facility-based and community-based risk assessment using an all-hazards approach. The current assessment identifies these priority hazards: [for example severe weather, flooding, extended power loss, IT outage, water disruption, active threat, infectious disease outbreak, supply chain disruption]. The assessment considers [the community hazard vulnerability analysis from the county emergency management agency or the regional healthcare coalition].

**Strategies.** For each priority hazard, Appendix A lists the strategy, the trigger for activating it, and who decides.

**Patient population and services.** During an emergency, [Organization name] [suspends elective procedures / completes procedures in progress and stabilizes patients for discharge or transfer]. [Describe what you can and cannot provide.]

**Continuity of operations.** If the [administrator] is unavailable, authority passes in this order: [title], [title], [title]. Critical functions and how they continue are listed in Appendix B.

**Cooperation with officials.** [Title] is the liaison with [county emergency management, the state health department, the regional healthcare coalition] and participates in [coalition meetings and exercises].

### 3. Policies and procedures

**Tracking staff and patients.** During an emergency the charge nurse keeps a list of on-duty staff and patients in the facility. If anyone is relocated, the charge nurse records the name and location of the receiving facility or other location.

**Evacuation.** The evacuation procedure in Appendix C covers the care and treatment needs of evacuees, staff responsibilities, transportation, evacuation locations, and primary and alternate ways to contact outside help.

**Shelter in place.** When evacuation is unsafe, patients, staff and volunteers shelter in [location], with [supplies] for [duration].

**Medical documentation.** Patient records are preserved, kept confidential and kept available during an emergency by [the EHR's downtime procedures, paper downtime forms, offsite backups].

**Volunteers and surge staffing.** [Organization name] [does or does not] use volunteers. State or federally designated health professionals are integrated by [process].

**Section 1135 waivers.** If the Secretary of Health and Human Services declares a waiver under section 1135 of the Social Security Act, [title] coordinates with emergency officials on any role for [Organization name] at an alternate care site.

### 4. Communication plan

Appendix D lists names and contact information for staff, entities providing services under arrangement, patients' physicians and volunteers, and contact information for federal, state, tribal, regional and local emergency preparedness staff and other sources of assistance.

- **Primary means of communication:** [phone, mass notification system]
- **Alternate means:** [text, two-way radio, satellite phone]
- **Sharing patient information** with other providers for continuity of care, and releasing information during an evacuation or about patients' general condition and location, is done as permitted by 45 CFR 164.510.
- **Reporting needs** to the authority having jurisdiction or the incident command center is done by [title].

### 5. Training and testing

**Training.** All new staff, contractors on site and volunteers receive emergency preparedness training at orientation, consistent with their roles. Everyone is retrained at least every 2 years, and whenever these policies change significantly. [Title] keeps training records and checks staff knowledge by [method].

**Testing.** [Organization name] tests the plan at least annually:

- Every 2 years, a full-scale, community-based exercise, or a facility-based functional exercise if a community exercise isn't available.
- In the opposite year, another exercise: a second full-scale or functional exercise, a mock drill, or a facilitated tabletop exercise.
- An actual emergency that activates the plan exempts [Organization name] from its next required full-scale or functional exercise.

After every exercise and real event, [title] documents what happened, analyzes the response, and revises the plan where needed.

### 6. Program review

[Title] reviews the emergency plan, policies and procedures, communication plan, and training and testing program at least every 2 years, and after any exercise or event that shows a gap. Each review is approved by [names and titles].

## How to adapt it

- **Start with a real risk assessment.** Surveyors look for a facility-specific assessment, not a generic list. Ask your county emergency management office or regional healthcare coalition for the community assessment.
- **Home health and hospice:** your sections add requirements for patients in their homes, such as individual emergency plans and following up with on-duty staff and patients. Hospices that run inpatient units have more still.
- **Your state may require more.** Some states have their own emergency plan rules for licensed facilities.

## Keep the two-year clock visible

In Bindersnap, each part of the program is a document in your binder, with the date and approvers of its current version on record. When the program is reviewed, the approvers sign off on that exact version, so a surveyor sees when it was last reviewed and by whom. Every earlier version stays, along with its approvals.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often must an emergency preparedness plan be reviewed?

For ambulatory surgery centers, home health agencies, hospices, and RHCs and FQHCs, at least every 2 years. The same applies to the policies and procedures, the communication plan, and the training and testing program.

### How often must we run an emergency exercise?

At least annually. An ASC must conduct a full-scale or functional exercise every 2 years and an additional exercise, such as a tabletop, in the opposite year (§416.54(d)(2)).

### Does a real emergency count as an exercise?

If an actual emergency requires activating the emergency plan, the ASC is exempt from its next required full-scale or facility-based functional exercise (§416.54(d)(2)(i)(B)).

Every other policy in your manual depends on this one. A **policy on policies**, also called a policy development and management policy, sets the rules for the manual itself: who may write a policy, who must approve it, how often it is reviewed, how staff learn about changes, and how long old versions are kept.

Surveyors and accreditors ask for it more often than people expect, usually right after they find a policy that is out of date. "Your policy says you review policies annually. This one was last reviewed in 2022."

## The rules it has to satisfy

No single federal rule describes a policy on policies, but several set requirements it must make room for:

| Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | What it means for your policy process                                                                     |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Governing body conditions: ASCs [§416.41](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-416.41), home health [§484.105](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-484.105), hospice [§418.100](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-418.100) | The governing body is responsible for the organization's policies, so your approval chain has to reach it |
| CMS emergency preparedness rules, such as [§416.54](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-416.54)                                                                                                                                                 | Emergency plans, policies and procedures reviewed and updated at least every 2 years                      |
| RHCs and FQHCs, [§491.9(b)(4)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-491.9)                                                                                                                                                                       | Patient care policies reviewed at least biennially by a group of professional personnel                   |
| Opioid treatment programs, [§8.12(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-8.12)                                                                                                                                                                 | Annual review of program policies and procedures                                                          |
| OSHA bloodborne pathogens, [29 CFR 1910.1030](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-1910.1030)                                                                                                                                                    | Exposure control plan reviewed and updated at least annually                                              |
| CLIA, [§493.1251(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-493.1251)                                                                                                                                                                              | Laboratory procedures and changes approved, signed and dated by the laboratory director before use        |
| HIPAA, [45 CFR 164.530(j)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-164.530)                                                                                                                                                                         | Privacy policies kept six years from creation or when last in effect                                      |

Your state and your accreditor may add their own review cycles. The simplest compliant rule is usually: **review every policy at least annually, and anything with a shorter required cycle on that cycle.**

## The template

### [Organization name] Policy Development, Review and Approval

- **Policy owner:** [Compliance Officer or Administrator]
- **Approved by:** [Governing body]
- **Effective date:** [Date]
- **Last reviewed:** [Date]

### 1. Purpose

To make sure the policies and procedures of [Organization name] are accurate, approved by the people responsible for them, available to the staff who follow them, reviewed on schedule, and kept as a record after they change.

### 2. Scope

All policies, procedures, plans and protocols of [Organization name], in every department, including those required by federal, state and accrediting rules.

### 3. Definitions

- **Policy:** A statement of what [Organization name] does and why.
- **Procedure:** The steps staff follow to carry out a policy.
- **Policy owner:** The person accountable for keeping a policy accurate and current.
- **Approver:** A person whose sign-off is required before a policy, or a change to it, takes effect.

### 4. Format

Every policy states its title, owner, approvers, effective date, last review date and next review date, and uses the standard sections: purpose, scope, definitions, policy, procedure, responsibilities and references.

### 5. Writing and revising a policy

1. Anyone may suggest a new policy or a change to the policy owner.
2. The policy owner drafts the new policy or revision, consulting the staff who will follow it.
3. The draft is proposed for approval. The current version stays in effect until the change is approved.

### 6. Approval

| Kind of policy                         | Required approvers                               |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Clinical policies                      | [Medical director and director of nursing]       |
| Administrative and HR policies         | [Administrator]                                  |
| Infection prevention policies          | [Infection prevention lead and medical director] |
| Laboratory procedures                  | [Laboratory director], before use                |
| Policies the governing body must adopt | Above approvers, then [the governing body]       |

A policy takes effect on the date it is approved, or on a later date stated in it. Each approval is recorded with the approver's name and the date, and kept with the exact version approved.

### 7. Communicating changes

Staff are told about new and changed policies [by email notice / at the next staff meeting / in the policy system] within [7 days] of approval. [Staff whose work a change affects acknowledge reading it within [14 days].] Training is given when a change alters how work is done.

### 8. Access

The current approved version of every policy is available to all staff [in the policy system]. Printed copies are uncontrolled and must be checked against the system before use.

### 9. Periodic review

Each policy is reviewed by its owner at least [annually], or more often when a law, regulation, accreditor or this table requires:

| Policy                                                                          | Review cycle           |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Emergency preparedness plan, policies, communication plan, training and testing | At least every 2 years |
| Exposure control plan                                                           | At least annually      |
| [Add others required for your organization]                                     | [Cycle]                |

A review with no changes is approved again by the required approvers, and the review date is updated. A policy is also reviewed whenever a regulation changes, after a survey finding, and after an incident that involves it.

### 10. Urgent changes

When a change cannot wait for the normal process (for example, a new public health directive), [the administrator and medical director] may approve an interim change. It goes through the full process within [30 days].

### 11. Retiring a policy and keeping old versions

A policy that is no longer needed is retired by the same approvers who would approve it. Every earlier version of every policy is kept, with its approvals and its dates in effect, for at least [six years after it was last in effect, or longer if state law or your accreditor requires], so [Organization name] can show what any policy said on any date.

### Approval

Reviewed and approved by [names and titles] on [date].

## How to adapt it

- **Fill in the approvals table honestly.** It's what surveyors test. Name roles your organization actually has.
- **Don't promise what you won't do.** If you can't collect staff acknowledgments for every change, require them only for the changes that alter how work is done.
- **Keep the review table short.** Only list policies whose cycle differs from your default.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should healthcare policies be reviewed?

There's no single federal interval for every policy. Specific rules set some cycles: annually for the OSHA exposure control plan and for opioid treatment program policies, and at least every 2 years for CMS emergency preparedness programs and RHC/FQHC patient care policies. Many organizations review every policy annually and follow the shorter cycle where one applies.

### Who should approve a policy on policies?

The governing body, since it is responsible for the organization's policies under the CMS conditions for ASCs, home health and hospice.

### How long should we keep old versions of policies?

At least six years after a policy was last in effect if you are a HIPAA covered entity. That is the HIPAA documentation rule, and most organizations apply it to every policy. Check your state's rules and your accreditor's standards for longer periods.

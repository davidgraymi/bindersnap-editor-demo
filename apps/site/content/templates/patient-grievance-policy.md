A patient who complains is giving you a chance to fix something before a surveyor finds it. CMS treats the grievance process as a patient right, and surveyors check two things: that your **written grievance procedure** has every element the rule lists, and that the grievances in your log were handled the way the procedure says.

For ambulatory surgery centers, the rule is [42 CFR §416.50(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-416.50). It is detailed enough to build a policy straight from, so this template follows it. Home health agencies (§484.50(e)) and hospices (§418.52(b)) have their own wording. Notes for both are at the end.

## What the ASC rule requires

The ASC "must establish a grievance procedure for documenting the existence, submission, investigation, and disposition of a patient's written or verbal grievance." In particular:

1. Alleged violations or grievances about mistreatment, neglect, or verbal, mental, sexual or physical abuse must be **fully documented**.
2. All allegations must be **immediately reported to a person in authority** in the ASC.
3. **Only substantiated allegations** must be reported to the state or local authority, or both.
4. The process must **specify timeframes** for reviewing the grievance and responding.
5. The ASC must **investigate all grievances** about treatment or care that is, or fails to be, furnished.
6. The ASC must document how each grievance was addressed and give the patient **written notice of its decision**, containing the name of a contact person, the steps taken to investigate, the result, and the date the process was completed.

Separately, under §416.50(a), the notice of rights given before the procedure must include the address and telephone number of the state agency where patients may report complaints, and the website of the Office of the Medicare Beneficiary Ombudsman.

## The template

### [Organization name] Patient Grievance Policy

- **Policy owner:** [Administrator]
- **Approved by:** [Governing body]
- **Effective date:** [Date]
- **Last reviewed:** [Date]

### 1. Purpose

To make sure every patient concern is heard, documented, investigated and answered, and that allegations of abuse or neglect are escalated immediately, in accordance with 42 CFR §416.50(d) and [state regulation].

### 2. Definitions

**Grievance:** A written or verbal complaint by a patient, the patient's representative or surrogate about treatment or care that is, or fails to be, furnished, or about mistreatment, neglect or abuse, that is not resolved at the time by staff present.

**Concern resolved at the time:** A complaint that staff present resolve to the patient's satisfaction immediately, such as a request for a blanket. It is logged as feedback, not as a grievance.

### 3. How patients file a grievance

Patients are told, in the notice of patient rights given before the procedure, that they may file a grievance with [Organization name] in person, by phone at [number], by email at [address] or in writing, and that they may also contact [the state agency, with address and phone number] or visit the website of the Office of the Medicare Beneficiary Ombudsman. Filing a grievance never affects a patient's care.

### 4. Receiving a grievance

Any staff member who receives a grievance:

1. Thanks the patient and writes down what was said, in the patient's words where possible.
2. Enters the grievance in the grievance log within [one business day].
3. If the grievance alleges mistreatment, neglect, or verbal, mental, sexual or physical abuse, **reports it immediately to [the administrator or designee]**, in person or by phone, before the end of their shift, and fully documents it.

### 5. Investigation

The [administrator or designee] assigns an investigator who was not involved in the matter. The investigator reviews the medical record, interviews staff and the patient as needed, and documents each step taken.

**Timeframes:**

- Acknowledge the grievance to the patient within [2 business days].
- Complete the investigation and send the written decision within [7 calendar days].
- If more time is needed, tell the patient in writing why and when to expect the decision, no later than [30 days] from receipt.

### 6. Reporting to authorities

Allegations of abuse or neglect that the investigation **substantiates** are reported to [the state survey agency and/or local authority] within [state-required timeframe]. [Add any state law that requires reporting before an allegation is substantiated, and mandatory reporting obligations for abuse of vulnerable adults.]

### 7. Written decision

The patient receives a written notice of the decision that contains:

- The name of the [Organization name] contact person
- The steps taken to investigate the grievance
- The result of the grievance process
- The date the grievance process was completed

A copy is kept with the grievance file.

### 8. Review and improvement

Grievances are reviewed [quarterly] by [the QAPI committee] for trends, and the governing body receives a summary [at each meeting]. Grievance files are kept for [retention period required by state law].

### Approval

Reviewed and approved by [names and titles] on [date].

## Notes for home health and hospice

- **Home health (§484.50(e)).** The HHA must investigate complaints from the patient, the representative, and the patient's caregivers and family. It must document both the complaint and how it was resolved, and take action to prevent further violations, **including retaliation**, while the complaint is investigated. Any staff member who notices mistreatment, neglect, abuse, injuries of unknown source or misappropriation of patient property must report it immediately to the HHA and to other authorities as state law requires.
- **Hospice (§418.52(b)).** Patients have the right to voice grievances about treatment or care and about lack of respect for property. Alleged violations involving mistreatment, neglect, abuse, injuries of unknown source or misappropriation of patient property must be **reported immediately to the hospice administrator** and investigated immediately. The hospice must act to prevent further violations while the alleged violation is being verified.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between a complaint and a grievance?

Most organizations treat a concern that staff resolve on the spot as a complaint or feedback, and anything that is not resolved at the time, or that alleges abuse or neglect, as a grievance that goes through the formal process. Define the line in your policy, as the template does.

### Does an ASC have to report every grievance to the state?

No. Under §416.50(d)(3), only substantiated allegations must be reported to the state authority or the local authority, or both. State law may require more, so check yours.

### What must the written grievance response include?

The name of an ASC contact person, the steps taken to investigate, the result of the grievance process, and the date the process was completed (§416.50(d)(6)).

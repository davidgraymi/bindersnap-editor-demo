## What a Q-tag is

When a surveyor finds that a surgery center has not met a requirement, the finding goes on Form CMS-2567 under a tag. For ambulatory surgery centers the tags start with Q and come from Appendix L of the CMS State Operations Manual, the guidance surveyors work from. Each tag stands for one piece of the Conditions for Coverage in 42 CFR Part 416: Q-0040 is the governing body condition as a whole, and Q-0042 is the hospital transfer standard inside it.

The first table above lists the Q-tags that turn on a written policy, procedure or program, with the document a surveyor reads under each. The second lists the tag for each condition.

## If you have been cited under one

1. **Read the rule the tag stands for.** Follow the link in the table. The finding has to describe a failure to meet those words, and a [plan of correction](/glossary/plan-of-correction) has to answer them.
2. **Find your policy for it, as it stood on the survey date.** A finding often turns on what the policy said that day. If the file has been saved over since, you are working from memory.
3. **Decide which problem you have.** There was no policy, or the policy was not approved by the people the rule names, or staff were not following it. Each needs a different correction.
4. **Write the plan of correction** for each deficiency, on the same form, and return it by the date in your cover letter. See [what a plan of correction must include](/requirements/what-a-plan-of-correction-must-include).

## Condition-level and standard-level

The tags in the second table are conditions, and the tags between them are the standards inside each one. The difference matters more than the numbering suggests. Whether a condition is met "depends upon the manner and degree to which the provider or supplier satisfies the various standards within each condition" ([42 CFR 488.26(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-488.26)). A center with deficiencies in standards can keep participating in Medicare if it submits an acceptable plan of correction ([42 CFR 488.28](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-488.28)). A condition found not met is the finding that puts certification at risk.

## Tags that name the approver

Three Q-tags ask who approved the policy as well as whether it exists:

- **Q-0060.** Surgeons hold privileges granted by the governing body "in accordance with approved policies and procedures of the ASC".
- **Q-0109.** The medical staff and the governing body together develop and revise the emergency equipment policies.
- **Q-0123.** Policies for overseeing practitioners other than physicians are "approved by the governing body".

For these, a current policy with no record of that approval is still a finding. See [who has to approve healthcare policies](/requirements/who-approves-policies).

## Frequently asked questions

### Where do Q-tags come from?

From Appendix L of the CMS State Operations Manual, "Guidance for Surveyors: Ambulatory Surgical Centers". It prints each tag above the part of 42 CFR Part 416 it stands for, followed by interpretive guidelines for the surveyor.

### Do accreditors use Q-tags?

No. AAAHC, The Joint Commission and QUAD A cite their own standards. Q-tags appear when a state agency surveys for CMS, including a validation survey after an accreditation survey.

### Which Q-tags cover emergency preparedness?

None. Emergency preparedness at §416.54 is cited under E-tags, which are the same for every kind of provider. See [the E-tags](/survey-tags/emergency-preparedness-e-tags).

## What a J-tag is

A rural health clinic's deficiencies are written on Form CMS-2567 under J-tags. The tags come from Appendix G of the CMS State Operations Manual, the guidance surveyors work from, and each stands for one piece of the conditions for certification in 42 CFR Part 491. J-0120 is the provision of services requirement as a whole. J-0123, J-0124 and J-0125 are the pieces of it about written patient care policies.

The first table above lists the J-tags that turn on a written policy, with the document a surveyor reads under each. The second lists the tag for each section of the rule.

## The three tags about patient care policies

Most of what a clinic is asked about its policies falls under three tags.

- **J-0123** covers the existence of written patient care policies, the group that develops them, and that group's review at least every two years. It is also where the physician's and the PA's or NP's part in writing the policies is cited.
- **J-0124** covers two things the policies must include: a description of the clinic's services, and guidelines for medical management.
- **J-0125** covers the third: rules for the storage, handling and administration of drugs and biologicals.

A clinic can have a full manual and still be cited under J-0123 if it can't show who was in the group, that one member came from outside the clinic's staff, and when the group last reviewed the policies.

## If you have been cited under one

1. **Read the rule the tag stands for.** Follow the link in the table. A [plan of correction](/glossary/plan-of-correction) has to answer those words.
2. **Find the policy, and its review record.** For J-0123 the record is the finding: the names, the date, and the version reviewed.
3. **Decide which problem you have.** No policy, a policy with no review on record, or a policy staff were not following.
4. **Write the plan of correction** for each deficiency, on the same form, and return it by the date in your cover letter. See [what a plan of correction must include](/requirements/what-a-plan-of-correction-must-include).

A clinic with deficiencies may continue in Medicare if it submits an acceptable plan of correction for achieving compliance within a reasonable period ([42 CFR 488.28](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-488.28)).

## Records tags

Two more tags turn on written policies. **J-0151** asks for a clinical record system kept "in accordance with written policies and procedures". **J-0153** asks for written policies and procedures on the use and removal of records and the conditions for releasing information. **J-0154**, beside them, is the six-year retention rule.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where do J-tags come from?

From Appendix G of the CMS State Operations Manual, "Guidance for Surveyors: Rural Health Clinics". It prints each tag above the part of 42 CFR Part 491 it stands for, followed by interpretive guidelines.

### Which J-tag covers the biennial policy review?

J-0123. The interpretive guidelines under it cover §491.9(b)(1), (2) and (4) together: written policies, the group that advises on them, and the review at least every two years.

### Which J-tags cover emergency preparedness?

None. Emergency preparedness at §491.12 is cited under E-tags, which are the same for every kind of provider. See [the E-tags](/survey-tags/emergency-preparedness-e-tags).

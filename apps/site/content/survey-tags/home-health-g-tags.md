## What a G-tag is

A home health survey ends with Form CMS-2567, and every deficiency on it sits under a G-tag. The tags come from Appendix B of the CMS State Operations Manual, the guidance surveyors work from, and each stands for one piece of the Conditions of Participation in 42 CFR Part 484. G406 is the patient rights condition as a whole. G462 is one standard inside it, the discharge-for-cause policy.

The first table above lists the G-tags that turn on a written policy or program, with the document a surveyor reads under each. The second lists the tag for each condition.

## If you have been cited under one

1. **Read the rule the tag stands for.** Follow the link in the table. A [plan of correction](/glossary/plan-of-correction) has to answer those words, not the surveyor's summary of them.
2. **Find your policy as it stood on the date in the finding.** Home health findings usually come from a record review or a home visit on a particular day. The question is what your policy said on that day, and whether the clinician followed it.
3. **Decide which problem you have.** No policy, a policy that was never approved, or a policy the field staff were not working from. In home health the third is the common one, because staff carry copies.
4. **Write the plan of correction** for each deficiency, on the same form, and return it by the date in your cover letter.

## The tags most tied to a document

- **G462, discharge for cause.** The agency may discharge for cause only "under a policy set by the HHA for the purpose". Without the policy, the discharge itself is the deficiency.
- **G584, verbal orders.** They may be accepted only by personnel authorized "by the HHA's internal policies". The surveyor reads the policy, then checks who took the order.
- **G990, acceptance to service.** The newest of them. The policy must be maintained "through an annual review", so the surveyor asks for the policy and for the date it was last reviewed.
- **G1026, record retention.** The agency's policies must say what happens to clinical records if it stops operating.

## Condition-level and standard-level

The tags in the second table are conditions, and the tags between them are the standards inside each one. Whether a condition is met "depends upon the manner and degree to which the provider or supplier satisfies the various standards within each condition" ([42 CFR 488.26(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-488.26)). Home health has its own enforcement rules in 42 CFR Part 488, Subpart J. Under them, a non-compliant agency "must submit a plan of correction for approval by CMS" whatever sanction applies ([42 CFR 488.810(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-488.810)).

## Frequently asked questions

### Where do G-tags come from?

From Appendix B of the CMS State Operations Manual, "Guidance to Surveyors: Home Health Agencies". It prints each tag above the part of 42 CFR Part 484 it stands for, followed by interpretive guidelines.

### Is it G-462 or G462?

People write it both ways. Appendix B prints the tag without a hyphen, as G462, and that is how this page lists them.

### Which G-tags cover emergency preparedness?

None. Emergency preparedness at §484.102 is cited under E-tags, which are the same for every kind of provider. See [the E-tags](/survey-tags/emergency-preparedness-e-tags).

## What an L-tag is

Every deficiency on a hospice's Form CMS-2567 is written under an L-tag. The tags come from Appendix M of the CMS State Operations Manual, the guidance surveyors work from, and each stands for one piece of the Conditions of Participation in 42 CFR Part 418. L686 is the whole condition on drugs, supplies and equipment. L694 is one standard inside it: the written policy on controlled drugs in the patient's home.

The first table above lists the L-tags that turn on a written policy, procedure or program, with the document a surveyor reads under each. The second lists the tag for each condition.

## If you have been cited under one

1. **Read the rule the tag stands for.** Follow the link in the table. A [plan of correction](/glossary/plan-of-correction) has to answer those words.
2. **Find your policy as it stood on the date in the finding.** Hospice findings come from clinical records, home visits and interviews, each tied to a date. What your policy said on that date is part of the answer.
3. **Decide which problem you have.** No policy, a policy nobody approved, or a policy the team was not following.
4. **Write the plan of correction** for each deficiency, on the same form, and return it by the date in your cover letter.

## Why so many hospice tags lead to a policy

The hospice conditions hand more decisions to the hospice's own policy than most. Read these tags and you find the phrase each time:

- **L554.** Coordination of services runs "in accordance with the hospice's own policies and procedures".
- **L584.** Licensed professionals "practice under the hospice's policies and procedures".
- **L663.** The hospice has "written policies and procedures describing its method(s) of assessment of competency".
- **L679.** Record entries are authenticated and dated "in accordance with hospice policy".
- **L694.** The hospice has "written policies and procedures for the management and disposal of controlled drugs in the patient's home".

Wherever the rule defers to your policy, the surveyor reads the policy first and then checks practice against it. A hospice is cited for not doing what its own document says.

## Condition-level and standard-level

The tags in the second table are conditions, and the tags between them are the standards inside each one. Whether a condition is met "depends upon the manner and degree to which the provider or supplier satisfies the various standards within each condition" ([42 CFR 488.26(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-488.26)). Hospice has its own enforcement rules in 42 CFR Part 488, Subpart N. Under them, a non-compliant hospice program "must submit a plan of correction for approval by CMS or the State Survey Agency" whatever remedy applies ([42 CFR 488.1210(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-488.1210)).

## Frequently asked questions

### Where do L-tags come from?

From Appendix M of the CMS State Operations Manual, "Guidance to Surveyors: Hospice". It prints each tag above the part of 42 CFR Part 418 it stands for, followed by interpretive guidelines.

### Is there an L-tag for discharge for cause?

Not in the conditions of participation. The discharge-for-cause policy is required by §418.26(a)(3), which sits with the election and discharge rules in Subpart B, outside the conditions Appendix M tags.

### Which L-tags cover emergency preparedness?

None. Emergency preparedness at §418.113 is cited under E-tags, which are the same for every kind of provider. See [the E-tags](/survey-tags/emergency-preparedness-e-tags).

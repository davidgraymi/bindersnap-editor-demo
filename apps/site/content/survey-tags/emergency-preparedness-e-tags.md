## What an E-tag is

CMS wrote one emergency preparedness rule and placed a copy of it in each provider's conditions: §416.54 for surgery centers, §418.113 for hospices, §484.102 for home health agencies, §491.12 for rural health clinics and FQHCs, and so on. Surveyors cite all of them under the same tags, which start with E and come from Appendix Z of the State Operations Manual. That is why a surgery center's statement of deficiencies can carry Q-tags and E-tags side by side.

The table above lists the E-tags that apply to all four kinds of provider on this site. The paragraph letter is the same in each provider's section, so E-0013 is paragraph (b) whether you read §416.54 or §491.12.

## Four documents answer most of them

The rule asks for a program with four parts. Each is a document of its own, each must be reviewed and updated at least every 2 years, and each has its tag.

| Document                                                                       | Tags it answers                |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Emergency plan, with the risk assessment it is based on                        | E-0004, E-0006, E-0007, E-0009 |
| Emergency policies and procedures                                              | E-0013                         |
| Communication plan                                                             | E-0029                         |
| Training and testing program, with its training records and exercise write-ups | E-0036, E-0037, E-0039         |

The [emergency preparedness policy template](/templates/emergency-preparedness-policy) covers the policies and procedures. The [glossary entry](/glossary/emergency-preparedness-program) explains the program as a whole.

## What surveyors ask to see

The review cycle is the part that leaves a paper trail. For each of the four documents a surveyor can ask when it was last reviewed and updated, and who did it. "At least every 2 years" is a date test. A plan last reviewed three years ago fails it, however good the plan is.

Three more things are records and not policies, and they are cited under the training and testing tags:

- **Training.** Documentation of the training given, and evidence that staff know the emergency procedures (E-0037).
- **Exercises.** The plan is tested with exercises, and the provider analyzes its response and revises the plan as needed (E-0039). How many exercises, and of what kind, differs by provider, so read your own section.
- **The risk assessment.** The plan must be "based on and include" a documented risk assessment using an all-hazards approach (E-0006). A plan with no assessment behind it is cited there.

## Tags that differ by provider

Appendix Z has more E-tags than this table. Many apply only to some providers, because the policies and procedures paragraph differs in its details. Home health agencies and hospices have tags of their own about their patients during an emergency, and inpatient providers have a tag about subsistence needs. This page lists only the tags whose paragraph is shared by surgery centers, hospices, home health agencies and rural health clinics. For the rest, read your own section of the rule against Appendix Z.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often must the emergency plan be reviewed?

At least every 2 years, for surgery centers, hospices, home health agencies and rural health clinics. The same cycle applies to the policies and procedures, the communication plan, and the training and testing program. Long-term care facilities are on an annual cycle.

### Are E-tags the same for every provider?

The numbering is. E-0004 is the emergency plan for every provider type in Appendix Z. Some tags apply only to certain providers, and a few details inside a shared tag differ, such as how often exercises are required.

### Where do E-tags come from?

From Appendix Z of the CMS State Operations Manual, "Emergency Preparedness for All Provider and Certified Supplier Types". It prints each tag above the list of sections it stands for, one per provider type.

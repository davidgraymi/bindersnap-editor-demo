**Deemed status** means CMS has certified a provider or supplier for Medicare participation because it is accredited by a CMS-approved accrediting organization, rather than because a state survey agency surveyed it. In CMS's words, it means "CMS has certified a provider or supplier for Medicare participation" after the provider "voluntarily applied for, and received, accreditation from a CMS-approved national accrediting organization" ([42 CFR 488.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-488.1)).

## How it works

An accrediting organization with CMS approval for a provider type, for example for ambulatory surgery centers, home health or hospice, surveys the provider against standards that meet or exceed the Medicare [Conditions of Participation](/glossary/conditions-of-participation) or [Conditions for Coverage](/glossary/conditions-for-coverage). If the provider is accredited under that program, it is "deemed" to meet the Medicare conditions. CMS keeps the right to survey deemed providers, for example to validate an accreditor's findings or to investigate a complaint.

## What it means for your policies

An accreditation survey reads your policy manual at least as closely as a state survey, and usually against more detailed standards. Accreditors also return between full surveys. Being ready on any day, not just in the months before a survey, is the practical meaning of [survey readiness](/glossary/survey-readiness).

## Related rules

The requirements for accrediting organizations, and what CMS can do when an accredited provider falls short, are in [42 CFR Part 488, Subpart A](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/part-488/subpart-A).

A **policy attestation**, also called a read-and-sign or a policy acknowledgment, is a record that a staff member read a policy, usually with their name and the date. Organizations collect attestations to show that staff were told about a policy or a change to it.

## Attestation vs. approval

The two are often confused, and they answer different questions:

|                     | Approval                                                                                                                         | Attestation                                                                                            |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Who                 | The people responsible for the policy, such as the medical director, administrator or governing body                             | The staff who must follow it                                                                           |
| Question it answers | "Is this the organization's policy?"                                                                                             | "Did this person read it?"                                                                             |
| Required by         | Organization rules, and some regulations directly (for example, CLIA requires the lab director to approve procedures before use) | Usually the organization's own policy, an accreditor's expectation, or a specific training requirement |
| When                | Before the policy or change takes effect                                                                                         | After it takes effect                                                                                  |

A policy without approval isn't the organization's policy, whoever has read it. An approved policy without attestations can still be followed. Surveyors usually test that through interviews and observation.

## Do you need attestations?

It depends on your accreditor, your state and your own policy on policies. Many small providers collect attestations at orientation and for high-risk changes, and rely on training records and observation for the rest.

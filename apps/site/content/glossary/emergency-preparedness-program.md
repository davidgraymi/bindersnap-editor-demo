An **emergency preparedness program** is the set of plans, policies and exercises that CMS requires Medicare-certified providers and suppliers to keep so they can protect patients and staff and keep operating, or shut down safely, in an emergency. Each provider type has its own section, for example [42 CFR 416.54](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-416.54) for ambulatory surgery centers, [484.102](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-484.102) for home health, [418.113](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-418.113) for hospice and [491.12](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-491.12) for RHCs and FQHCs.

## The four parts

1. **Emergency plan**, based on a documented facility-based and community-based risk assessment using an all-hazards approach.
2. **Policies and procedures** based on the plan, the risk assessment and the communication plan.
3. **Communication plan**: contacts and primary and alternate means of communication.
4. **Training and testing program**: initial and recurring training, and exercises to test the plan.

For the provider types above, each part must be reviewed and updated **at least every 2 years**. Testing runs on its own clock: an ASC, for example, must test its plan at least annually.

## Keeping it current

The program fails surveys most often on dates: a plan last reviewed three years ago, or no record of the exercise.

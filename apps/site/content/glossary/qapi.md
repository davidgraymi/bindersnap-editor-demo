**QAPI** stands for **quality assessment and performance improvement**: an ongoing, data-driven program in which a healthcare organization measures its own performance, finds problems, and fixes them. CMS requires one in the conditions for several provider types:

- **Ambulatory surgery centers:** "an ongoing, data-driven quality assessment and performance improvement (QAPI) program" ([42 CFR 416.43](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-416.43)).
- **Home health agencies:** "an effective, ongoing, HHA-wide, data-driven QAPI program" ([42 CFR 484.65](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-484.65)).
- **Hospices:** "an effective, ongoing, hospice-wide data-driven quality assessment and performance improvement program" ([42 CFR 418.58](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-418.58)).

In each case the governing body is responsible for the program.

## How QAPI connects to policies

QAPI and the policy manual feed each other:

- **Monitoring checks policies.** A hand hygiene audit, a chart review or an infection rate measures whether staff follow a policy. Our [hand hygiene template](/templates/hand-hygiene-policy) includes the monitoring section that reports to QAPI.
- **Fixes change policies.** When QAPI finds a problem, the fix is often a revised policy plus training. The same is true of a [plan of correction](/glossary/plan-of-correction) after a survey.
- **The record shows the loop closed.** A surveyor follows a QAPI finding to the policy revision, and asks when it was approved and by whom.

Bindersnap keeps each policy revision with its approval dates and approvers, so the link from a QAPI finding to the changed policy is on record.

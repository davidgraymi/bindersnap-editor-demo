The **governing body** of a healthcare organization is the board, owners or other group that holds legal responsibility for how the organization runs. Under the Medicare conditions, that responsibility explicitly covers the organization's policies.

## What the rules say

- **Ambulatory surgery centers:** the governing body "assumes full legal responsibility for determining, implementing, and monitoring policies governing the ASC's total operation" ([42 CFR 416.41](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-416.41)).
- **Home health agencies:** a governing body, or designated persons functioning as one, "must assume full legal authority and responsibility for the agency's overall management and operation" ([42 CFR 484.105(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-484.105)).
- **Hospices:** a governing body, or designated persons functioning as one, "assumes full legal authority and responsibility for the management of the hospice" ([42 CFR 418.100(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-418.100)).

In a small organization the governing body may be two or three owners, often including the medical director.

## The governing body and your policy manual

The governing body rarely approves every procedure. It usually approves a [policy on policies](/templates/policy-review-and-approval) that says who may approve what, adopts the most important policies itself, and oversees the quality program. Surveyors check that this delegation is written down and followed. See [who has to approve healthcare policies](/requirements/who-approves-policies).

**Conditions of Participation (CoPs)** are the federal health and safety requirements that a healthcare provider must meet to take part in Medicare and Medicaid. CMS defines them as "the requirements providers other than skilled nursing facilities must meet to participate in the Medicare program," and the definition includes the conditions for certification that rural health clinics meet ([42 CFR 488.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-488.1)).

## Who they apply to

Each type of provider has its own set of CoPs in Title 42 of the Code of Federal Regulations. For example:

- Home health agencies: [42 CFR Part 484](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/part-484)
- Hospices: [42 CFR Part 418](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/part-418)
- Rural health clinics and FQHCs: [42 CFR Part 491, Subpart A](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/part-491/subpart-A)

_Suppliers_, such as ambulatory surgery centers, meet [Conditions for Coverage](/glossary/conditions-for-coverage) instead. They work the same way.

## How they're checked

A state survey agency, acting for CMS, surveys the provider against its CoPs, or an accrediting organization with CMS approval does so for providers with [deemed status](/glossary/deemed-status). Deficiencies are written up on a [statement of deficiencies](/glossary/statement-of-deficiencies), and the provider answers with a [plan of correction](/glossary/plan-of-correction).

## Why they matter for your policies

Many CoPs require written policies outright, such as hospice controlled drug disposal (§418.106) or emergency preparedness policies reviewed every two years (§484.102, §418.113). Others are proven through policies, such as patient rights and infection control. The provider pages on this site list them: [home health](/for/home-health-agencies), [hospice](/for/hospices) and [rural health clinics](/for/rural-health-clinics).

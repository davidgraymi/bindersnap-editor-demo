## How to use this index

Most home health agencies start with a policy manual bought from a template vendor or written by a consultant before the first survey. The manual is long, and the agency changes around it. A few years on, nobody is sure which of its policies the Conditions of Participation ask for and which were only ever in the template.

The table above answers that. It lists what 42 CFR Part 484 asks of a home health agency's policies, grouped the way a manual is usually bound, with the rule for each row and the G-tag a surveyor would cite on a [statement of deficiencies](/glossary/statement-of-deficiencies).

Download the Word file to work through it. It has the same rows and three empty columns: your own document's name, who approved it and when, and when it is next due. An empty cell is a gap to close.

## Two kinds of row

- **The rule asks for it in writing.** The condition names a policy, a procedure, a plan or a program. The discharge-for-cause policy at §484.50(d)(5) is one: an agency may discharge a patient for cause only "under a policy set by the HHA for the purpose".
- **Met through a written policy.** The condition sets a requirement and leaves the method to you. §484.50(e) says complaints must be investigated and documented. It doesn't say "complaint policy", and no surveyor expects an agency to do it without one.

## The newest row

The patient acceptance-to-service policy at §484.105(i) was added by a rule published in November 2024, so a manual written before then doesn't have it. The agency must "develop, implement, and maintain through an annual review" a policy, applied the same way to every referral, that addresses its capacity to provide care: the anticipated needs of the patient, its caseload and case mix, its staffing levels, and the skills and competencies of its staff. It is also the only row outside emergency preparedness where the rule itself sets a review cycle. If your manual predates it, start here.

## What this index leaves out

It is the federal floor for a home health policy manual, not the whole manual.

- **Your accreditor.** CHAP, ACHC and The Joint Commission each have standards of their own, many of which call for policies not listed here. Their standards are copyrighted and not reproduced.
- **Your state.** States that license home health add policies and often set review cycles.
- **Rules for every employer or covered entity.** The OSHA exposure control plan and the HIPAA privacy and security policies apply to an agency too. The [required policies checklist](/tools/required-policies-checklist) lists them.
- **Clinical procedures.** §484.75 says skilled professionals practice according to the agency's policies and procedures. It doesn't list them, because they follow from the services you offer.

## Who approves the manual

Under §484.105(a) the governing body "must assume full legal authority and responsibility for the agency's overall management and operation". In most agencies the administrator and the clinical manager approve policies and the governing body adopts them. Whatever your chain is, write it down in a policy on policies and follow it, because the surveyor's question is whether the approval matched your own rule. See [who has to approve healthcare policies](/requirements/who-approves-policies).

## Frequently asked questions

### What policies does a home health agency need for a Medicare survey?

At the federal level, the ones in this table: governing body and administration, personnel policies, the acceptance-to-service policy, patient rights including transfer, discharge and complaints, verbal orders, the QAPI and infection control programs, record retention, and the four parts of the emergency preparedness program. Your state and accreditor add more.

### What is a G-tag?

A G-tag is the number CMS gives each home health requirement in Appendix B of the State Operations Manual, the guidance surveyors use. G640 is the QAPI condition, for example. A deficiency on Form CMS-2567 is written under the tag of the requirement that wasn't met.

### How often must a home health agency review its policies?

Two cycles are in the conditions. The acceptance-to-service policy is maintained through an annual review (§484.105(i)), and the emergency plan, its policies and procedures, the communication plan and the training and testing program are each reviewed and updated at least every 2 years (§484.102). For the rest, your accreditor or state sets the cycle. See [how often policies must be reviewed](/requirements/how-often-to-review-policies).

### Is a purchased home health policy manual enough?

It is a starting point. A manual has to be adopted: checked against what your agency does, changed where it differs, and approved under your own approval chain. A policy the staff don't follow is cited as readily as one that is missing.

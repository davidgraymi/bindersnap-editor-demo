## How to use this index

The rules for a rural health clinic are short, and they are unusually exact about policies. They say what the patient care policies must contain, who must help write them, and how often that group must review them. A clinic can meet every clinical expectation and still be cited because the group never met, or met and left no record.

The table above lists what 42 CFR Part 491 asks for in writing, grouped the way a clinic's manual is usually bound, with the rule for each row and the J-tag a surveyor would cite on a [statement of deficiencies](/glossary/statement-of-deficiencies). The same part of the rules applies to federally qualified health centers.

Download the Word file to work through it. It has the same rows and three empty columns: your own document's name, who approved it and when, and when it is next due. An empty cell is a gap to close.

## The three things the patient care policies must include

§491.9(b)(3) lists them, and each is a row above:

1. **A description of the services** the clinic furnishes directly and those it furnishes through agreement or arrangement.
2. **Guidelines for medical management** of health problems, including the conditions that require consultation or referral, the maintenance of health care records, and how the clinic's services are periodically reviewed and evaluated.
3. **Rules for the storage, handling and administration of drugs and biologicals.**

A manual that has all three, each traceable to an approved version, answers most of what a surveyor reads under J-0124 and J-0125.

## The group, and the review every two years

Two requirements trip clinics more than the content does.

- **Who writes them.** The policies are "developed with the advice of a group of professional personnel that includes one or more physicians and one or more physician assistants or nurse practitioners", and at least one member is not on the clinic's staff (§491.9(b)(2)). The outside member is the one clinics forget.
- **How often.** That same group reviews the policies at least biennially (§491.9(b)(4)). Separately, the clinic evaluates its total program every two years, and that evaluation includes whether the established policies were followed (§491.11).

Both leave the same evidence: the names of the people who reviewed, the date, and the version they reviewed. See [who has to approve healthcare policies](/requirements/who-approves-policies).

## What this index leaves out

It is the federal floor for a rural health clinic policy manual, not the whole manual.

- **Your state.** State law governs scope of practice for PAs and NPs, and §491.9(b)(1) requires the policies to be consistent with it.
- **Your accreditor, if you have one.** The Compliance Team and QUAD A accredit rural health clinics and have their own standards.
- **Rules for every employer or covered entity.** The OSHA exposure control plan and the HIPAA privacy and security policies apply to a clinic too, and so does CLIA for the laboratory tests §491.9(c)(2) requires. The [required policies checklist](/tools/required-policies-checklist) lists them.

## Frequently asked questions

### What policies does a rural health clinic need?

In writing, under 42 CFR Part 491: the organization's policies and lines of authority, patient care policies covering the three subjects in §491.9(b)(3), policies for the clinical record system and for the use, removal and release of records, a biennial program evaluation, and the four parts of the emergency preparedness program. Your state adds more.

### What is a J-tag?

A J-tag is the number CMS gives each rural health clinic requirement in Appendix G of the State Operations Manual, the guidance surveyors use. J-0123 covers the written patient care policies and the group that develops them. A deficiency on Form CMS-2567 is written under the tag of the requirement that wasn't met.

### Who has to be in the group that reviews RHC policies?

One or more physicians, one or more physician assistants or nurse practitioners, and at least one member who is not on the clinic's staff (§491.9(b)(2)). They review the policies at least every two years.

### How long must a rural health clinic keep patient records?

At least 6 years from the date of the last entry, and longer if state law requires it (§491.10(c)).

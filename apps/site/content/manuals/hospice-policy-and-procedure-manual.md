## How to use this index

The hospice Conditions of Participation mention the hospice's "own policies" more often than the rules for almost any other provider. Authentication of record entries follows hospice policy. Coordination of services follows the hospice's own policies and procedures. Who may order restraint is set by hospice policy. Each of those phrases is a document a surveyor can ask to read.

The table above collects them. It lists what 42 CFR Part 418 asks of a hospice's policies, grouped the way a manual is usually bound, with the rule for each row and the L-tag a surveyor would cite on a [statement of deficiencies](/glossary/statement-of-deficiencies).

Download the Word file to work through it. It has the same rows and three empty columns: your own document's name, who approved it and when, and when it is next due. An empty cell is a gap to close.

## Two kinds of row

- **The rule asks for it in writing.** The condition names a policy, a procedure, a plan or a program. The clearest is §418.106(e)(2)(i): the hospice "must have written policies and procedures for the management and disposal of controlled drugs in the patient's home", and must hand them to the family when the drugs are first ordered.
- **Met through a written policy.** The condition sets a requirement and leaves the method to you. The governing body's responsibility under §418.100(b) is one. Nothing says "policy on policies", and a surveyor will still ask how the governing body exercises it.

## Rows that apply only to some hospices

Three rows open with "If you". They apply when the hospice has more than one interdisciplinary group, or provides inpatient care directly in its own facility. A hospice that provides inpatient care only under arrangement with a hospital or nursing facility can strike the inpatient rows. It then needs a written agreement that the inpatient provider's patient care policies are consistent with its own (§418.108(c)).

## The policy that leaves the building

Most policies are read by staff. The controlled drug policy is read by families. The rule requires the hospice to give a copy to the patient or representative and the family, discuss it in a language and manner they understand, and document in the clinical record that it did. So the version handed over on a given date has to be recoverable. If the policy has been revised since, you need the earlier version to show what the family was given. There is a [controlled drug disposal policy template](/templates/controlled-drug-disposal-policy) to start from.

## What this index leaves out

It is the federal floor for a hospice policy manual, not the whole manual.

- **Your accreditor.** CHAP, ACHC and The Joint Commission have their own standards, many calling for policies not listed here. Those standards are copyrighted and not reproduced.
- **Your state.** State hospice licensing rules add policies and often set review cycles.
- **Rules for every employer or covered entity.** The OSHA exposure control plan and the HIPAA privacy and security policies apply to a hospice too. See the [required policies checklist](/tools/required-policies-checklist).
- **Clinical protocols.** §418.62 says licensed professionals practice under the hospice's policies and procedures, without listing them.

## Frequently asked questions

### What policies are required for a Medicare-certified hospice?

At the federal level, the ones in this table. Those the conditions name outright include advance directives, competency assessment, controlled drugs in the patient's home, authentication and retention of clinical records, discharge for cause, the QAPI and infection control programs, the bereavement program, and the four parts of the emergency preparedness program. Your state and accreditor add more.

### What is an L-tag?

An L-tag is the number CMS gives each hospice requirement in Appendix M of the State Operations Manual, the guidance surveyors use. L559 is the QAPI condition, for example. A deficiency on Form CMS-2567 is written under the tag of the requirement that wasn't met.

### How often must a hospice review its policies?

The conditions set one cycle: the emergency plan, its policies and procedures, the communication plan and the training and testing program are each reviewed and updated at least every 2 years (§418.113). For the rest, your accreditor or state sets the cycle, and most hospices review every policy annually. See [how often policies must be reviewed](/requirements/how-often-to-review-policies).

### How long must a hospice keep clinical records?

Six years after the death or discharge of the patient, unless state law requires longer (§418.104(d)). The same standard says hospice policies must provide for retention and storage of records if the hospice stops operating.

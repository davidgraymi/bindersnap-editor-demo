## How to use this index

A surgery center's policy and procedure manual usually arrives one of two ways: bought as a template, or inherited from whoever ran the center before. Either way, the question a surveyor brings is the same. Is there a policy for each thing the rules ask for, and can you show who approved the version in force?

The table above lists what the Medicare Conditions for Coverage ask of an ambulatory surgery center, in the order of the conditions themselves, which is also how most ASC manuals are bound. Each row gives the rule, in its own words where they are short enough, and the Q-tag a surveyor would cite on a [statement of deficiencies](/glossary/statement-of-deficiencies).

Download the Word file to work through it. It has the same rows, with three empty columns: the name of your own document, who approved it and when, and when it is next due for review. A row you can't fill in is a gap to close before the survey.

## Two kinds of row

Each row says which kind it is.

- **The rule asks for it in writing.** The condition names a policy, a procedure, a plan or a program. The history and physical policy at §416.52(a)(1) is the clearest case: the ASC "must develop and maintain a policy". If the document doesn't exist, the condition isn't met.
- **Met through a written policy.** The condition sets a requirement and leaves the method to you. Nothing in §416.46 says "nursing policy", but it does say patient care responsibilities must be delineated for all nursing personnel, and a surveyor will ask where.

The difference matters when time is short. Write the first kind first.

## What this index leaves out

It covers the federal conditions and nothing else, so it is the floor of a surgery center policy manual and not the whole of it.

- **Your accreditor.** AAAHC, The Joint Commission and QUAD A each publish their own standards, and many call for policies this list doesn't have. Their standards are copyrighted, so they are not reproduced here.
- **Your state.** State licensing rules for surgery centers often add policies and set their own review cycles. §416.40 makes compliance with them a condition of its own.
- **Rules for every employer or every covered entity.** The OSHA exposure control plan and the HIPAA privacy and security policies apply to you too. The [required policies checklist](/tools/required-policies-checklist) lists those.
- **Clinical procedures.** The conditions ask for surgical and anesthesia services to follow approved policies. They don't list the procedures, because those depend on the cases you do.

## Who approves the manual

Under §416.41 the governing body "assumes full legal responsibility for determining, implementing, and monitoring policies governing the ASC's total operation." Three rows go further and name the governing body or the medical staff as the approver: emergency equipment at §416.44(d), oversight of practitioners other than physicians at §416.45(c), and privileges at §416.42. For those, a signature from the administrator alone doesn't answer the rule. See [who has to approve healthcare policies](/requirements/who-approves-policies).

## Keeping the index true

An index is correct on the day it is filled in. It goes wrong when a policy is revised and the approval column isn't. In Bindersnap, each row is a document in a binder with named approvers. A revision takes effect only when they approve it, and the earlier version stays on record with its own approvals, so the answer to "who approved this, and when?" is on the page. ## Frequently asked questions

### What should a surgery center policy and procedure manual include?

At the federal level, a policy, procedure, plan or program for each Condition for Coverage in 42 CFR Part 416, Subpart C: governing body, surgical services, QAPI, environment, medical staff, nursing, medical records, pharmaceutical services, laboratory and radiologic services, patient rights, infection control, admission and discharge, and emergency preparedness. The table on this page lists each one with its rule. Your state and your accreditor add to it.

### What is a Q-tag?

A Q-tag is the number CMS gives each ASC requirement in Appendix L of the State Operations Manual, the guidance surveyors work from. Q-0040 is the governing body condition, for example. A deficiency on Form CMS-2567 is written under the tag of the requirement that wasn't met.

### How often does an ASC have to review its policies?

The conditions set one cycle: the emergency plan, its policies and procedures, the communication plan and the training and testing program are each reviewed and updated at least every 2 years (§416.54). Privileges are reappraised "periodically" (§416.45(b)). For the rest, your accreditor or state usually sets the cycle, and most centers review everything annually. See [how often policies must be reviewed](/requirements/how-often-to-review-policies).

### Can I buy an ASC policy manual instead of writing one?

Yes, and many centers do. A purchased manual still has to be adopted: reviewed against what your center does, changed where it differs, and approved by your governing body. Surveyors cite policies that staff don't follow as readily as policies that are missing.

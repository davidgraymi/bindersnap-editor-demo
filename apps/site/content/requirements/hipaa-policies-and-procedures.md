**Short answer:** a HIPAA covered entity must have **written privacy and security policies and procedures**, change them **promptly** when the law changes, **document** every change, **review** its security documentation periodically, make the policies **available** to the people who carry them out, and keep each version for **six years** from when it was created or last in effect, whichever is later.

This page is about the rules for the policies themselves. It doesn't cover what each policy must say.

## The rules, one by one

### Have policies, sized to your organization

"A covered entity must implement policies and procedures with respect to protected health information that are designed to comply with" the Privacy Rule. They must be "reasonably designed, taking into account the size and the type of activities" of the organization ([45 CFR 164.530(i)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-164.530)). The Security Rule has a parallel requirement for electronic protected health information ([45 CFR 164.316(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-164.316)).

A five-person practice doesn't need a hospital's manual. It does need written policies that fit what it actually does.

### Change them when the law changes

A covered entity "must change its policies and procedures as necessary and appropriate to comply with changes in the law," and when a change in law requires a change, it "must promptly document and implement the revised policy or procedure" (§164.530(i)(2)–(3)). Other changes may be made at any time, as long as they are documented and implemented.

If a change affects the practices described in your Notice of Privacy Practices, the notice has to be revised too (§164.530(i)(4)).

### Keep them in writing

Privacy policies and procedures must be maintained "in written or electronic form" (§164.530(j)(1)), and security policies "in written (which may be electronic) form" (§164.316(b)(1)). Electronic is fine. A policy that exists only as "how we do it" is not.

### Keep every version for six years

Documentation must be retained "for six years from the date of its creation or the date when it last was in effect, whichever is later" (§164.530(j)(2); §164.316(b)(2)(i)). That applies to each version: the one you replaced last year must be kept until six years after it was replaced.

### Make them available, and review them

Under the Security Rule, documentation must be available to the people responsible for carrying out the procedures, and reviewed "periodically", then updated "as needed, in response to environmental or operational changes" affecting the security of electronic protected health information (§164.316(b)(2)(ii)–(iii)).

## A checklist for your HIPAA policy manual

- Written privacy policies and procedures covering your uses and disclosures of PHI
- Written security policies and procedures for ePHI, based on your risk analysis
- A named privacy official and security official
- A record of every change, with the date it took effect
- Each version kept for six years after it was last in effect
- Policies available to the staff who follow them
- A periodic review cycle, written down and followed
- A process to update policies, and the Notice of Privacy Practices, when the law changes

## Frequently asked questions

### Does HIPAA require policies to be reviewed annually?

HIPAA doesn't set an annual cycle. The Security Rule requires periodic review of its documentation, and both rules require prompt changes when the law changes. Many covered entities review annually because it's simple to show.

### Can HIPAA policies be kept electronically?

Yes. HIPAA requires policies and procedures to be maintained in written or electronic form.

### How long must old HIPAA policies be kept?

Six years from the date the version was created or last in effect, whichever is later.

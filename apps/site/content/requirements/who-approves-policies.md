**Short answer:** for Medicare-certified providers, the **governing body** is ultimately responsible for the organization's policies. A few rules go further and name who must approve a specific kind of policy, such as the laboratory director for CLIA procedures, and a professional group that includes an outside member for rural health clinic policies. Everyone else sets their own approval chain in a policy on policies, and surveyors check that it was followed.

## What the rules say about approval

| Who                             | What the rule says                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Rule                                                                         |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ASC governing body              | "assumes full legal responsibility for determining, implementing, and monitoring policies governing the ASC's total operation"                                                                                            | [42 CFR 416.41](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-416.41)        |
| Home health governing body      | "must assume full legal authority and responsibility for the agency's overall management and operation"                                                                                                                   | [42 CFR 484.105(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-484.105)   |
| Hospice governing body          | "assumes full legal authority and responsibility for the management of the hospice"                                                                                                                                       | [42 CFR 418.100(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-418.100)   |
| RHC and FQHC professional group | Policies are developed with the advice of a group that includes one or more physicians, one or more PAs or NPs, and at least one member who is not on the clinic's staff, and that group reviews them at least biennially | [42 CFR 491.9(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-491.9)       |
| CLIA laboratory director        | "Procedures and changes in procedures must be approved, signed, and dated by the current laboratory director before use"                                                                                                  | [42 CFR 493.1251(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-493.1251) |
| OTP medical director            | Assumes responsibility for all medical and behavioral health services the OTP performs, and for its compliance with federal, state and local law                                                                          | [42 CFR 8.12(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-8.12)         |

## A typical approval chain for a small provider

Most small providers split the work like this, and write it into their policy on policies:

| Kind of policy                     | Approved by                                                    |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Clinical policies and procedures   | Medical director and director of nursing (or clinical manager) |
| Infection prevention               | Infection prevention lead and medical director                 |
| Administrative and HR              | Administrator                                                  |
| Laboratory procedures              | Laboratory director, before use                                |
| Policies the governing body adopts | The above, then the governing body                             |

The governing body doesn't need to read every procedure. It needs a process it has approved, and evidence that the process was followed. That's why surveyors ask, "Who approved this, and when?"

## What makes an approval hold up

An approval is good evidence when it is:

1. **Tied to the exact text.** "The board approved the infection control policy in March" is weak if the policy has changed three times since.
2. **By the right person.** The approver named in your policy on policies, not whoever was available.
3. **Dated, and recorded before use.** CLIA says so outright, and every surveyor assumes it.
4. **Kept.** The record of who approved a version must survive the next version.

## How Bindersnap records approvals

Each binder names its approvers. A change to a policy is proposed, and it becomes the policy only when the required approvers approve it. Each approval is recorded with the person and the date against that exact version, and every earlier version keeps its own approvals. See [how a change becomes the record](/help/approvals). Reviewers approve without a paid seat, so you can name the medical director, the lab director or an outside RHC group member without paying for them.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the board have to approve every policy?

CMS makes the governing body responsible for the organization's policies, but most organizations have the board approve a policy on policies and the most important policies, and delegate the rest to named leaders. Write the delegation down, and follow it.

### Who signs laboratory procedures?

The current laboratory director must approve, sign and date each procedure, and each change to it, before use (42 CFR 493.1251(d)).

### Who must review rural health clinic policies?

A group of professional personnel including at least one physician, at least one PA or NP, and at least one person who isn't on the clinic's staff, at least every two years (42 CFR 491.9(b)).

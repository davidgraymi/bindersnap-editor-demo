**Short answer:** no federal rule sets one review interval for every healthcare policy. A handful of rules set specific cycles, from "before use" to "at least every 2 years". Your state licensing rules and your accreditor often add more. Most small providers review every policy at least annually and follow a shorter cycle wherever one applies, because that rule is the easiest to keep and to prove.

## The review cycles federal rules set

| What                                                                                      | Who it applies to                                                        | Cycle                                                                       | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Exposure control plan                                                                     | Any employer with occupational exposure to blood                         | At least annually, and when tasks or positions change                       | [29 CFR 1910.1030(c)(1)(iv)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-1910.1030)                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Program policies and procedures                                                           | Opioid treatment programs                                                | Annual review, as part of the quality assurance plan                        | [42 CFR 8.12(c)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-8.12)                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Emergency plan, policies and procedures, communication plan, training and testing program | ASCs, home health, hospice, RHCs and FQHCs, and other Medicare providers | At least every 2 years                                                      | [42 CFR 416.54](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-416.54), [484.102](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-484.102), [418.113](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-418.113), [491.12](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-491.12) |

| Laboratory procedures                                                                     | CLIA-certified laboratories                                              | Approved, signed and dated by the director before use, and for every change | [42 CFR 493.1251(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-493.1251)                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| HIPAA security documentation                                                              | HIPAA covered entities                                                   | Reviewed periodically and updated as needed                                 | [45 CFR 164.316(b)(2)(iii)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-164.316)                                                                                                                                                                                        |

Other conditions say only that policies must be current and followed. One example is reappraising ASC medical staff privileges "periodically" ([§416.45](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-416.45)). For those, your accreditor's standards and your own policy on policies set the cycle.

## What counts as a review

A review isn't just opening the file. Surveyors look for evidence that:

1. The right person read the policy against current practice and current rules.
2. Any changes were approved by the people who must approve them.
3. The review is dated, and a policy with no changes was still signed off.

A policy whose footer says "Reviewed: 2023" fails the third test even if someone did read it last year.

## Why "annually" is the usual answer

- It meets every federal cycle above except "before use", and the CLIA rule is triggered by each change anyway.
- Many state licensing rules and accreditor standards expect annual review of at least some policies.
- One cycle for everything is easier to track, and easier to prove, than a different one per policy.

If you adopt an annual cycle, say so in your [policy on policies](/templates/policy-review-and-approval), list the exceptions with shorter cycles, and then keep to it. A written cycle you miss is worse than none.

## Keeping the review on record

In Bindersnap, a review is an approval. The reviewer approves the current version, and their name and the date are recorded against that exact version, even when nothing in it changed. Every earlier version stays with its approvals. To show a surveyor your review history, you open the policy.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does CMS require annual policy review?

Not as one rule for every policy. CMS sets specific cycles for some programs, such as at least every 2 years for emergency preparedness and for RHC/FQHC patient care policies. Your state and accreditor may require annual review of more.

### Does OSHA require annual review of any policy?

Yes. The bloodborne pathogens exposure control plan must be reviewed and updated at least annually (29 CFR 1910.1030(c)(1)(iv)).

### Do opioid treatment programs have to review policies every year?

Yes. An OTP's quality assurance and quality control plans must include annual reviews of program policies and procedures (42 CFR 8.12(c)(1)).

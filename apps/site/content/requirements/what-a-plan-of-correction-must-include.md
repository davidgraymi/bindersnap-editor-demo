**Short answer:** CMS tells state survey agencies that an acceptable plan of correction "must contain" four elements: the plan for correcting the specific deficiency, the procedure for implementing it, the monitoring procedure that keeps it corrected, and the title of the person responsible. It must give a completion date, be specific, and be signed and dated by the administrator or another authorized official. You have 10 calendar days from receiving the Form CMS-2567 to return it. This page covers providers other than nursing homes, which have rules of their own.

## The four elements

They come from the State Operations Manual, §2728B, the instructions CMS gives the agencies that survey for it.

| Element                | In CMS's words                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The correction         | "The plan of correcting the specific deficiency. The plan should address the processes that lead to the deficiency cited"                                                                |
| How it is carried out  | "The procedure for implementing the acceptable plan of correction for the specific deficiency cited"                                                                                     |
| How it stays corrected | "The monitoring procedure to ensure that the plan of correction is effective and that specific deficiency cited remains corrected and/or in compliance with the regulatory requirements" |
| Who is responsible     | "The title of the person responsible for implementing the acceptable plan of correction"                                                                                                 |

The same section adds what the entry on the form itself needs:

- **A date.** The plan "must include the provider/supplier's planned action to correct the deficiency and the expected completion date".
- **Specifics.** Plans "must be specific and realistic, stating exactly how the deficiency was or will be corrected".
- **A signature.** It "must be signed and dated by the administrator or other authorized official".

A title, not a name, goes in the fourth element. The form becomes public, and the manual says a plan may not "malign an individual" or refer to another provider.

## The deadlines

| What                                       | When                                                  | Source                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| The state agency sends you the CMS-2567    | Within 10 working days after the survey               | SOM §2728                                                                |
| You return it with your plan of correction | Within 10 calendar days of receiving it               | SOM §2728B                                                               |
| The deficiencies are corrected             | Ordinarily within 60 days of being notified           | [42 CFR 488.28(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-488.28) |
| The form is available to the public        | Within 90 calendar days of the last day of the survey | SOM §2728B                                                               |

Your cover letter sets your actual dates. Follow it where it differs.

Sixty days is the outside expectation, not a default. The manual tells agencies not to accept 60-day dates routinely "when the deficiency can reasonably be corrected well before". The rule itself gives an example of when more time is fair, and it is about policies: when "a facility must obtain the approval of its governing body".

## When the finding is about a policy

Many findings are. Either there was no written policy where the rule asks for one, or the policy was never approved by the people who must approve it, or staff were not following it. For each, the four elements read differently.

1. **The correction** names the policy written or revised, and what changed in it. "Policy updated" is not specific. "Grievance procedure revised to add a 7-day timeframe for written response" is.
2. **The procedure** says how the revision became the policy: who drafted it, who approved it, the date it took effect, and how staff were told.
3. **The monitoring** says what will be checked, how often and by whom, and where the results go. For a policy finding this is usually an audit of records against the new policy, reported to the QAPI program.
4. **The responsible title** is whoever owns that policy.

At the revisit, the surveyor asks to see what the plan promised: the revised policy, its approval and its effective date. If the approval date is later than the completion date you wrote on the form, the form is wrong.

## If you disagree with a finding

The manual gives a provider three options: accept the deficiencies and submit a plan; record objections on the form and submit a plan anyway; or record objections, submit no plan, and provide "convincing arguments and documented evidence that the deficiencies are invalid".

Two limits apply. You can dispute whether the finding is accurate, and you cannot dispute the surveyor's judgment about how serious it is. And if the agency rejects your arguments and you still submit no acceptable plan, it recommends termination. Most providers who object choose the second option.

## Home health agencies and hospices

42 CFR 488.28 excludes home health agencies and hospices, which have their own enforcement rules. Both still require a plan of correction: a non-compliant home health agency "must submit a plan of correction for approval by CMS" ([42 CFR 488.810(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-488.810)), and a non-compliant hospice program must submit one "for approval by CMS or the State Survey Agency" ([42 CFR 488.1210(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-488.1210)).

## Frequently asked questions

### How long do you have to submit a plan of correction?

Ten calendar days from receiving the Form CMS-2567, under the State Operations Manual. Your state agency's letter gives your actual date.

### Is writing "corrected" enough?

No. The manual requires plans to state "exactly how the deficiency was or will be corrected". If a deficiency was fixed after the survey, say what was done and give the approximate date.

### Can a plan of correction be changed after it is submitted?

Yes. The manual lets a provider submit evidence of correction or a modified plan to the state agency at any time. If the agency finds a plan unacceptable, it rejects it in writing and asks for another.

### Does submitting a plan of correction admit the deficiency?

The form gives you room to record objections and still submit a plan. Whether to add a statement about admission is a question for your own counsel.

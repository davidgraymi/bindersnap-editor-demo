**Short answer:** if you are a HIPAA covered entity, keep your privacy and security policies, and every earlier version of them, for **six years from the date each version was created or last in effect, whichever is later**. Laboratory procedures under CLIA must be kept for **two years after they are discontinued**. State law and your accreditor may require longer. Most providers apply the six-year rule to every policy, because sorting policies by retention rule costs more than keeping them.

## The federal retention rules for policies

| What                                                                                          | Keep for                                                             | Rule                                                                                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| HIPAA privacy policies and procedures, and any action or designation they require in writing  | 6 years from creation or the date last in effect, whichever is later | [45 CFR 164.530(j)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-164.530)                                                                                |
| HIPAA security policies and procedures, and required documentation of actions and assessments | 6 years from creation or the date last in effect, whichever is later | [45 CFR 164.316(b)(2)(i)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-164.316)                                                                          |
| CLIA test procedures, with the dates of initial use and discontinuance                        | At least 2 years after the procedure was discontinued                | [42 CFR 493.1105(a)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-493.1105) and [493.1251(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-493.1251) |
| OSHA bloodborne pathogens training records                                                    | 3 years from the date of training                                    | [29 CFR 1910.1030(h)(2)(ii)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-1910.1030)                                                                     |

The HIPAA clock is easy to misread. It runs from when a version was **last in effect**, not from when it was written. A privacy policy adopted in 2015 and replaced in 2024 must be kept until 2030.

## Why you want every version, not just the current one

Retention rules are the minimum. The practical reason to keep every version is that **every question about the past is a question about an old version**:

- A surveyor follows up on a 2023 complaint and asks what your policy said then.
- A former employee's lawyer asks which version of the attendance policy applied when they were disciplined.
- An incident review needs to know whether staff followed the procedure in force on the day, not today's.

A policy manual that is saved over each time can't answer any of these. Neither can one where old versions sit in an "archive" folder with no record of when each one applied or who approved it.

## What a usable policy history includes

For each version of each policy:

1. The exact text.
2. The date it took effect, and the date it was replaced or retired.
3. Who approved it.
4. What changed from the version before.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does HIPAA require policies to be kept?

Six years from the date of creation or the date the policy was last in effect, whichever is later (45 CFR 164.530(j)(2) for privacy; 164.316(b)(2)(i) for security).

### Do we need to keep policies that were retired?

Yes. A retired policy was in effect until it was retired, and the six-year HIPAA clock starts on that date.

### How long must a lab keep old procedures?

At least two years after a procedure is discontinued, with the dates it was first used and discontinued (42 CFR 493.1105(a)(2)).

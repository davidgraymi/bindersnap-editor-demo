**Short answer:** usually not a signature as such. The Medicare conditions for surgery centers, home health agencies, hospices and rural health clinics make someone responsible for policies and sometimes say who must approve them. They don't say a policy must carry a handwritten signature. The clear exception is the laboratory: under CLIA, procedures "must be approved, signed, and dated by the current laboratory director before use". What every surveyor does ask for is evidence of who approved which version, and when.

## What the rules say

| Rule                                                                                                              | Its words                                                                                                                | A signature?                    |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------- |
| CLIA procedure manual, [42 CFR 493.1251(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-493.1251)               | "Procedures and changes in procedures must be approved, signed, and dated by the current laboratory director before use" | Yes, by name                    |
| ASC practitioners other than physicians, [42 CFR 416.45(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-416.45) | "established policies and procedures, approved by the governing body"                                                    | Approval, no mention of signing |
| ASC governing body, [42 CFR 416.41](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-416.41)                         | "assumes full legal responsibility for determining, implementing, and monitoring policies"                               | Responsibility                  |
| Home health governing body, [42 CFR 484.105(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-484.105)            | "full legal authority and responsibility for the agency's overall management and operation"                              | Responsibility                  |
| Hospice governing body, [42 CFR 418.100(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-418.100)                | "full legal authority and responsibility for the management of the hospice"                                              | Responsibility                  |
| RHC patient care policies, [42 CFR 491.9(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-491.9)                 | Developed with the advice of a named group, which reviews them at least biennially                                       | Review by a group               |
| HIPAA policies, [45 CFR 164.530(j)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-164.530)                        | Maintained "in written or electronic form"                                                                               | No                              |

Where CMS wants a signature it says so. The home health clinical records rule requires each entry to be authenticated with "a signature and a title (occupation), or a secured computer entry by a unique identifier" (§484.110(b)). That rule is about patient records, not policies. No equivalent sentence exists for an agency's policies.

## So why does every manual have a signature page?

Because the rules ask for something a signature page is an old way of proving. A governing body that is responsible for policies has to be able to show it acted. An accreditor's standards, a state licensing rule or your own policy on policies may go further and require a signature outright. If your own policy says "each policy is signed by the administrator and the medical director", a surveyor holds you to it, whatever the federal rule says.

The trouble with the page is what it leaves out:

- **Which text was signed.** A signature page at the front of a binder doesn't say which wording of the policy behind it was approved. Revise the policy and the old signature appears to cover the new text.
- **When.** A signature without a date can't show the policy was approved before it was used.
- **Whether the signer had authority.** The page shows a name. It doesn't show that the name was the approver your policy on policies requires.

What makes an approval hold up is the same on paper or on a screen: it is tied to the exact text, by the right person, dated before use, and kept after the next version replaces it.

## Electronic approval

Nothing in the Medicare conditions above says an approval must be on paper, and HIPAA says outright that its policies may be kept in electronic form. Your state or accreditor may say more, so check their wording for the word "signature" and for any rule about electronic records.

For laboratory procedures, read the rule as written: "signed". If your laboratory director approves procedures electronically, ask your CLIA surveyor or your laboratory accreditor whether they accept that record as the director's signature, and keep a signed copy until they have said yes.

## What Bindersnap records, and what it isn't

In Bindersnap a change to a policy is approved by the people you name as approvers. Each approval is recorded with the person, the date and the exact version approved, and earlier versions keep their own approvals. That answers the three gaps above: which text, when, and by whom.

It is a record of approval. It is not a drawn signature, and it is not an electronic signature system under FDA's 21 CFR Part 11. ## Frequently asked questions

### Does CMS require policies to be signed?

Not in the conditions for surgery centers, home health agencies, hospices or rural health clinics. They make the governing body responsible and, in places, require its approval. CLIA, which CMS also administers, does require the laboratory director to sign and date procedures before use.

### Who signs a policy and procedure manual?

Whoever your policy on policies names. In most small providers that is the administrator and the medical director or director of nursing for clinical policies, with the governing body adopting the manual. See [who has to approve healthcare policies](/requirements/who-approves-policies).

### Does a policy need to be re-signed after an annual review with no changes?

It needs a dated record that the right person reviewed it. A footer that says "Reviewed 2023" with no name or approval behind it is weak evidence. See [how often policies must be reviewed](/requirements/how-often-to-review-policies).

### Does a new laboratory director have to re-sign every procedure?

The rule says procedures are approved, signed and dated by "the current laboratory director". Ask your CLIA surveyor or accreditor how they apply that when the director changes.

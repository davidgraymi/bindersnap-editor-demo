Hospice policies are read in some of the hardest moments in health care: a nurse explaining to a family how to dispose of morphine after a death, an aide working a night visit alone, an on-call physician deciding on a medication change. Those moments need the current policy. A survey or a complaint investigation then needs proof of which version was in force and who approved it.

Bindersnap keeps every hospice policy with its current approved version, every earlier version, and the name and date of each approval.

## What the hospice Conditions of Participation say

Medicare-certified hospices meet the Conditions of Participation in [42 CFR Part 418](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/part-418). Under §418.100, a governing body "assumes full legal authority and responsibility for the management of the hospice." Some conditions go further and name a written policy outright. Under §418.106, for example, the hospice "must have written policies and procedures for the management and disposal of controlled drugs in the patient's home." It must also give a copy to the patient or family when controlled drugs are first ordered.

Hospices accredited by The Joint Commission, CHAP or ACHC meet those organizations' standards too, and states license hospices under their own rules.

## Where hospice policy manuals go wrong

- **The family handout and the policy disagree.** The controlled drug disposal sheet was updated, but the policy behind it wasn't, or the other way round.
- **Approvals are informal.** The medical director agreed to a change in an IDG meeting, and the only record is a line in the minutes.
- **Home health and hospice policies blur.** Agencies that run both keep near-duplicate policies that drift apart.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a hospice need a written policy on disposing of controlled drugs?

Yes. Under 42 CFR §418.106, a hospice must have written policies and procedures for managing and disposing of controlled drugs in the patient's home, and must give a copy to the patient or representative and family when controlled drugs are first ordered.

### How often do hospice emergency preparedness policies need review?

At least every 2 years. Under §418.113, the emergency plan and the emergency preparedness policies and procedures must be reviewed and updated at least every 2 years.

### We run home health and hospice. Can we keep both manuals?

Yes. Create a binder for each, with its own approvers. Binders are never priced: your organization pays for the people who write policies, once each, however many binders they work in. See [pricing](/pricing).

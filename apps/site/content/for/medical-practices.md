A physician practice or outpatient clinic doesn't have a single Conditions of Participation rulebook the way a surgery center or a home health agency does. It has several smaller ones: OSHA for staff safety, HIPAA for privacy and security, CLIA if you run lab tests, and often a payer, a state board or an accreditor on top. Each one requires something in writing, and each one can ask to see it.

Bindersnap keeps all of those policies in one place, each with its current approved version, every earlier version, and who approved each one and when.

## The written policies the rules require

| Rule                                                                                                   | What has to be in writing                                          | Review and retention                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens, [29 CFR 1910.1030](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-1910.1030) | An Exposure Control Plan                                           | Reviewed and updated at least annually, and when tasks or positions change                                         |
| OSHA Hazard Communication, [29 CFR 1910.1200](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-1910.1200) | A written hazard communication program for each workplace          | Kept current with the chemicals on site                                                                            |
| OSHA Emergency Action Plans, [29 CFR 1910.38](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/section-1910.38)   | An emergency action plan, where another OSHA standard requires one | In writing for employers with more than 10 employees                                                               |
| HIPAA Privacy Rule, [45 CFR 164.530](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-164.530)            | Privacy policies and procedures                                    | Kept 6 years from creation or when last in effect                                                                  |
| HIPAA Security Rule, [45 CFR 164.316](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-164.316)           | Security policies and procedures                                   | Reviewed periodically; kept 6 years                                                                                |
| CLIA, [42 CFR 493.1251](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-493.1251)                        | A procedure manual for each test                                   | Approved, signed and dated by the laboratory director before use; old procedures kept 2 years after discontinuance |

The CLIA rule is worth reading twice: "Procedures and changes in procedures must be approved, signed, and dated by the current laboratory director before use." Every change needs a recorded approval, and the laboratory must keep each procedure with the dates it was first used and discontinued.

## Where practice policy manuals go wrong

- **The plan exists, but not the review.** The exposure control plan is in a binder, and nobody can show it was reviewed this year.
- **Policies are spread across systems.** HIPAA policies are in the compliance officer's folder, OSHA in the office manager's, lab procedures at the analyzer.
- **Old versions vanish.** HIPAA wants six years of documentation, and the practice has only the current file.

## How Bindersnap keeps the record

- **Every policy in one binder, or one per area.** OSHA, HIPAA, lab and clinical policies, each with the right approvers.
- **Recorded approvals.** A change becomes the policy only when the people you choose approve it, such as the physician owner, the lab director or the practice manager. Their names and the date stay with that version.
- **Nothing is saved over.** Every earlier version stays, so six years of HIPAA documentation is already kept for you.
- **Annual reviews in minutes.** Re-approve a policy you've reviewed and the review is on record. Export it all to PDF or Word with [the audit packet](/help/exporting).

Bindersnap holds your practice's policies and procedures, never patient records.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often does OSHA require the exposure control plan to be reviewed?

At least once a year. Under 29 CFR 1910.1030(c)(1)(iv), the exposure control plan must be reviewed and updated at least annually, and whenever new or modified tasks, procedures or positions affect occupational exposure.

### How long must a practice keep old HIPAA policies?

Six years from the date the policy was created or the date it was last in effect, whichever is later, under 45 CFR 164.530(j) and 164.316(b)(2).

### Who must approve a CLIA procedure?

The current laboratory director must approve, sign and date each procedure and each change to it before it is used (42 CFR 493.1251(d)).

Behavioral health programs answer to more rulebooks than almost anyone: state licensing, SAMHSA for opioid treatment, federal confidentiality rules for substance use disorder records, and an accreditor such as CARF or The Joint Commission. Each one reads your policy manual. Each one wants to know that the policy is current, that the right people approved it, and that staff follow it.

Bindersnap keeps every policy with its current approved version, every earlier version, and who approved each one and when.

## Rules that name your policies directly

**Opioid treatment programs (OTPs).** Federal opioid treatment standards in [42 CFR §8.12](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/section-8.12) require an OTP to "maintain current quality assurance and quality control plans that include, among other things, annual reviews of program policies and procedures." The same section requires a current Diversion Control Plan as part of the quality assurance program. If you run an OTP, an annual review of every policy is not optional, and you need to show it happened.

**Substance use disorder records under Part 2.** A program covered by [42 CFR Part 2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/part-2) "must have in place formal policies and procedures to reasonably protect against unauthorized uses and disclosures of patient identifying information" (§2.16). Those policies are what an auditor asks for after a breach.

**HIPAA.** Most behavioral health providers are also covered entities under HIPAA, which requires written privacy and security policies and procedures and keeping them for six years from when they were created or last in effect ([45 CFR §164.530](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-164.530) and [§164.316](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/section-164.316)).

**State licensing and accreditation.** States license outpatient, residential and crisis programs under their own rules, and CARF and The Joint Commission accredit many programs. Their standards differ in detail, but all expect written policies that leadership has approved and staff follow.

## The policies programs are most often asked for

| Area                     | Typical policies                                                               |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Admission and assessment | Eligibility, intake, assessment, level of care                                 |

## How Bindersnap keeps the record

- **Annual review you can prove.** When you review a policy, approve it again in Bindersnap. The approval is recorded with names and dates against that exact version.
- **Approvals before anything changes.** Revisions are proposed and reviewed by the approvers you name, such as the medical director or the program director, and only then become the policy. See [how a change becomes the record](/help/approvals).
- **Every version kept.** Open the policy as it was on any date, with its approvals.
- **Reviewers are free.** Clinical leads can review and approve without a paid seat. See [reviewing a change](/help/reviewing).

Bindersnap holds your program's policies and procedures. It is not for client records, and you should never put patient identifying information or Part 2 records in it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do opioid treatment programs have to review policies every year?

Yes. Under 42 CFR §8.12(c), an OTP's quality assurance and quality control plans must include annual reviews of program policies and procedures. Keep a record of each review: who reviewed the policy, when, and which version they approved.

### What policies does 42 CFR Part 2 require?

Section 2.16 requires a Part 2 program to have formal policies and procedures that protect records against unauthorized use and disclosure and against reasonably anticipated threats. Those cover paper and electronic records, transferring and removing records, destroying them, and maintaining them.

### How long should we keep old versions of our policies?

If you're a HIPAA covered entity, keep your privacy and security policies for six years from the date they were created or last in effect, whichever is later. Bindersnap keeps every version for as long as you use it.

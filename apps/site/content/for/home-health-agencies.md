A home health agency's staff work alone, in patients' homes, far from the office binder. That makes the policy manual more important, not less. The aide at the bedside and the nurse doing the start-of-care visit both need the current version, and when a surveyor arrives, the administrator needs to show which version was in force and who approved it.

Bindersnap gives every policy one current version that everyone reads from any device, and keeps each earlier version with the record of who approved it.

## What the Conditions of Participation say

A Medicare-certified home health agency meets the Conditions of Participation in [42 CFR Part 484](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/part-484). Under §484.105, a governing body "must assume full legal authority and responsibility for the agency's overall management and operation." The policies that put that responsibility in writing are what surveyors read first.

| Condition                                | Policies a surveyor will look for                                                      |
| ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| §484.50 Patient rights                   | Notice of rights, how complaints are investigated and resolved, discharge and transfer |
| §484.55 Comprehensive assessment         | Initial and comprehensive assessments, OASIS timing                                    |
| §484.60 Care planning and coordination   | Plans of care, physician orders, coordination between disciplines                      |
| §484.65 QAPI                             | A data-driven, agency-wide quality program                                             |
| §484.70 Infection prevention and control | An infection control program with documented practices                                 |
| §484.80 Home health aide services        | Aide training, competency evaluation and supervision                                   |
| §484.102 Emergency preparedness          | An emergency plan and policies, reviewed and updated at least every 2 years            |
| §484.105 Organization and administration | Governing body, administrator, clinical manager, contracted services                   |
| §484.110 Clinical records                | Record content, retention, protection                                                  |

Most agencies are also accredited by The Joint Commission, CHAP or ACHC, and every state that licenses home health has its own rules. They differ in the details. All of them expect written policies approved by the people responsible, and staff who follow the current version.

## Where home health policy manuals go wrong

- **Field staff read old copies.** A policy printed for orientation two years ago is still in a clinician's car.
- **Revisions pile up unapproved.** The clinical manager rewrites the bag technique procedure, emails it around, and it's never formally adopted.
- **Nobody can reconstruct the past.** After a complaint, you need to know what the policy said on the date of the visit, and the file has been saved over.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often must a home health agency review its policies?

The Conditions of Participation set a specific interval for emergency preparedness: the plan and its policies and procedures must be reviewed and updated at least every 2 years (§484.102). For most other policies, CMS expects them to be current and followed, and your accrediting organization or state may set a review cycle. Many agencies review every policy each year so nothing falls through.

### Who should approve home health policies?

Under §484.105 the governing body is responsible for the agency's operation. In practice the administrator and the clinical manager approve most policies, and the governing body adopts them. In Bindersnap you choose the approvers for each binder.

### Can aides and nurses read policies without paying for seats?

Yes. Staff who only read, and reviewers who approve, are free. Only the people who write and publish policies are paid seats, at $39 a month each. See [pricing](/pricing).

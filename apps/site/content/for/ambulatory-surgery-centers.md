An ambulatory surgery center lives by its policy manual. Every survey, whether it is a state licensing visit, a CMS validation survey or an accreditation survey, starts from the same place: the surveyor reads a policy, watches the staff, and asks whether the two match. Then they ask who approved the policy and when.

Bindersnap keeps that answer with the policy. Each policy has its current approved version, every earlier version, and the record of who signed off on each one.

## What the rules say about your policies

If your ASC is certified by Medicare, it meets the Conditions for Coverage in [42 CFR Part 416, Subpart C](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/part-416/subpart-C). The first of them sets the tone for the rest. Under §416.41, the governing body "assumes full legal responsibility for determining, implementing, and monitoring policies governing the ASC's total operation." A policy nobody can show was approved by that governing body is a weak policy.

The conditions that most often turn into written policies are:

| Condition                                              | What you'll be asked to show                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| §416.41 Governing body and management                  | Board approval of policies, contracted services, hospital transfer arrangements          |
| §416.42 Surgical services                              | Anesthesia and surgical policies, pre-procedure evaluation, discharge                    |
| §416.43 Quality assessment and performance improvement | A QAPI program the governing body oversees                                               |
| §416.44 Environment                                    | Safety, sanitation and fire-safety policies                                              |
| §416.45 Medical staff                                  | Privileging, and periodic reappraisal of privileges                                      |
| §416.48 Pharmaceutical services                        | Drug storage, administration and adverse-reaction policies                               |
| §416.50 Patient rights                                 | Notice of rights, advance directives, a written grievance procedure                      |
| §416.51 Infection control                              | An infection control program that seeks to minimize infections and communicable diseases |
| §416.52 Admission, assessment and discharge            | Pre-surgical assessment and discharge policies                                           |
| §416.54 Emergency preparedness                         | An emergency plan, policies and procedures, reviewed at least every two years            |

Most ASCs are also accredited, by AAAHC, The Joint Commission or another accrediting organization, and many states license ASCs under their own rules. Each adds its own standards, but every one of them asks for the same thing underneath: written policies, approved by the right people, followed by staff, and kept current.

## Where ASC policy manuals go wrong

The policies are usually fine. The record around them is not.

- **Nobody can say which version is current.** The binder at the nurses' station says one thing, the shared drive says another, and the copy emailed to the medical director says a third.
- **Approvals live in email.** The governing body approved the new infection control policy at a meeting, the minutes say so, and nobody can tie the minutes to the exact words that were approved.
- **The history is gone.** When a surveyor asks what the policy said on the date of an incident, the only answer is the current file, saved over the old one.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does CMS require ASC policies to be reviewed every year?

Not as a single rule for every policy. The Conditions for Coverage make the governing body responsible for the ASC's policies (§416.41) and set a specific interval for some programs: the emergency plan and its policies and procedures must be reviewed and updated at least every two years (§416.54). Other conditions, such as reappraising medical staff privileges (§416.45), say only "periodically". Your state and your accrediting organization may require more. Many ASCs review every policy annually because it's the simplest rule to follow.

### Who has to approve an ASC policy?

The governing body is responsible for the ASC's policies, and most ASCs have the medical director and the administrator or director of nursing approve clinical policies before the governing body adopts them. Bindersnap lets you name the approvers for each binder, so a change can't become the policy until they sign off.

### Can we keep using our Word documents?

Yes. Upload the Word and PDF files you have today and each one becomes a document with its own version history. You can also write new policies in Bindersnap's editor.

## How to use this checklist

Tick every line above that describes your organization. The table shows the written policies, plans and programs that **federal** rules require of an organization like yours, how often each must be reviewed or how long it must be kept, and the rule it comes from. Follow "How to write it" for a template or a guide.

Every row comes from the current text of the Code of Federal Regulations, linked in the last column.

## What this list doesn't cover

This is the federal floor, not your whole manual:

- **State law.** Your state's licensing rules usually require more policies than the federal rules do, and some set their own review cycles.
- **Your accreditor.** AAAHC, The Joint Commission, CHAP, ACHC, CARF and others have their own standards, many of which call for written policies.
- **Policies the rules imply.** Many conditions are met _through_ policies without requiring one by name. Surgical services, medical records and patient admission and discharge are examples. Surveyors expect written policies for them anyway.
- **Employment law.** Policies required for every employer, such as anti-harassment, are outside this list.

Use it to check that nothing federal is missing, then add what your state and accreditor require.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which policies are required for a medical office?

At the federal level, it depends on what you do. If staff could be exposed to blood, OSHA requires an exposure control plan, reviewed annually. If staff work with hazardous chemicals, OSHA requires a written hazard communication program. If you are a HIPAA covered entity, you need privacy and security policies and procedures. If you run lab tests, CLIA requires a procedure manual approved by the lab director. Your state may require more.

### Does this list include Joint Commission requirements?

No. Accreditation standards are copyrighted and vary by program. This list covers federal regulations only, with links to their text.

### How often is this list updated?

It is checked against the current eCFR text when the page's date changes. Rules change, so follow the citation to confirm before you rely on a row.

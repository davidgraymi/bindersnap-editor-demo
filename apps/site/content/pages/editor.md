**Write your policies in Bindersnap, in your browser. Nobody needs Microsoft Word to write one, review one or read one.**

A lot of policy software is built around Word. You write the policy in Word, check the file in, and the policy system stores it. So every author needs an Office license on top of the policy system's subscription, and the system can't see inside the file it's supposed to be keeping a record of. The reviewer downloads it, opens it in whatever their computer has, and emails back a comment about "the third paragraph on page two".

Bindersnap has a word processor of its own. It opens on the policy's page, it looks and works the way Word does, and every word you write in it is part of the record.

## It works the way Word does

The person who keeps your policy manual has spent years in Word. The editor doesn't ask them to learn anything new:

- **A ribbon of commands** over a white page: Home, Insert, View, and a Table tab when you're in a table.
- **Fonts, sizes and colors**, including Calibri, Cambria, Arial, Garamond and Times New Roman, with highlighting, shading, bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, subscript and superscript.
- **Headings and numbered sections.** Four levels of headings, numbered the way a policy manual numbers them, and a table of contents you insert from the ribbon.
- **Lists and checklists**, with indenting, line spacing and paragraph spacing.
- **Tables** you can resize, with header rows, merged and split cells, and rows and columns inserted where you need them.
- **Pictures** with alt text, links, symbols, the date and time, horizontal lines and page breaks.
- **Find and replace**, an outline of every heading, word and page counts in the status bar, and a print layout.
- **The shortcuts you already know**: Ctrl+B, Ctrl+S, Tab to indent, Format Painter, change case, curly quotes and dashes as you type.

## Nothing to install, nothing to license

The editor runs in the browser you already have, on the computer you already have. There's no Office add-in, no plugin, and no desktop program to keep up to date. Your medical director can review a policy from the laptop at home. A reviewer at a rural clinic doesn't need IT to install anything first.

You pay $39 a month for each person who writes and publishes policies, and that includes the editor. Reviewers and staff who only read are free, and none of them need a Word license either. See [pricing](/pricing).

## Every save is a draft, not the policy

Writing in the editor never changes the approved version. Each save goes into your own draft, which nobody else sees until you propose it. When you propose it, your approvers read the change on the policy's page, ask their questions there, and approve it or ask for changes. Only then does it become the policy, with who approved it and when. See [how a change becomes the record](/help/approvals).

That's the difference from writing in Word. A Word file is a copy. Somebody can edit it, save it under a new name and send it on, and the policy system never knows. A policy written in Bindersnap has one current version, and the only way to change it is a new version that goes through review.

## Reviewers see exactly what changed

Because the policy is written in Bindersnap, the comparison can read every word of it. A reviewer opens the change and sees the policy as it will read, with the words that were added and removed marked inside it. Nobody has to compare two printouts side by side, or trust that the author listed everything they touched.

## Bring the Word files you already have

You don't have to retype your manual. Upload your Word and PDF files as they are, and Bindersnap versions and approves them exactly as they are. When you want to keep editing one in Bindersnap, choose **Edit in Bindersnap** on its page: the file is converted into an editor document as its next version, in your draft, and reviewed like any other change. You can also tick **Make it editable in Bindersnap** as you upload. Nothing is converted unless you ask.

If your team would rather keep writing in Word, that works too. Every new version you upload goes through the same review and approval. See [adding documents](/help/adding-documents).

## Hand it to anyone

A policy written in the editor downloads as a PDF or a Word document from its page, whenever you need to hand it to a surveyor, a new hire or a board member. Your record is yours, and exporting it stays free whether or not you're subscribed. See [PDF, Word and the audit packet](/help/exporting).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need Microsoft Word to use Bindersnap?

No. You can write, review, approve and read every policy in your browser. Word is only needed if you choose to keep writing your policies in Word and uploading them.

### Does the editor work like Word?

Yes, on purpose. It has a ribbon, a white page, headings and numbered sections, tables, pictures, find and replace, an outline of the headings and the keyboard shortcuts people already know.

### Can I turn a Word file into a policy I edit in Bindersnap?

Yes. Choose Edit in Bindersnap on a Word or PDF document's page, or tick Make it editable in Bindersnap as you upload it. The converted version is reviewed like any other change before it becomes the policy.

### Can I still download a policy as a Word file?

Yes. A policy written in Bindersnap downloads as a PDF or a Word document from its page.

### Is the editor an extra cost?

No. It's part of the $39 a month for each person who writes policies. Reviewers and readers are free.
